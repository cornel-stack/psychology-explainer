# SOURCES — "The Psychology of People Who Imagine Fake Scenarios All Day"

Channel: **Dear Brain**. A portrait of **immersive daydreamers** — people with a
vivid, ongoing inner world — and where it crosses into **maladaptive daydreaming
(MD)**, a proposed condition coined by **Prof. Eli Somer (2002)**. Warm and
non-pathologising: a rich imagination is a gift; MD is when it starts to *cost* you.
All art is bespoke.

## The 9 traits (immersive-daydreamer tendencies)
| # | Trait | Basis | Source |
|---|-------|-------|--------|
| 1 | **A whole second world** | Vivid, detailed, cinematic inner scenes — a mind's-eye movie. | Fantasy proneness (Wilson & Barber); absorption (Tellegen). |
| 2 | **Ongoing sagas & characters** | Elaborate, serialised plots with recurring, beloved characters that develop over years. | Somer; MD phenomenology. |
| 3 | **Music is the on-switch** | Music is the most common trigger; it scores and sustains the daydream. | Somer et al. (triggers of MD). |
| 4 | **Pacing & movement** | Repetitive movement — pacing, rocking, gestures — accompanies the daydream. | MD stereotyped-movement reports. |
| 5 | **Real feelings for unreal people** | Genuine joy, grief, love toward imagined characters/events. | MD emotional-immersion studies. |
| 6 | **Hours vanish** | Deep absorption; losing large chunks of time (sometimes hours). | Somer; MDS-16. |
| 7 | **An escape & a comfort** | Used to cope with boredom, loneliness, anxiety, or past trauma. | MD linked to coping/dissociation. |
| 8 | **A richer alter-ego life** | An idealised parallel self/life — often better than reality. | Fantasy & self research. |
| 9 | **When it tips over (maladaptive)** | It interferes with sleep, work, relationships; feels compulsive, hard to stop; causes distress. | Somer's MD criteria; MDS-16. |

## Key clarifications (handled in VO)
- **Not psychosis.** Daydreamers **know** it isn't real — the opposite of a
  hallucination/delusion. This is crucial and stated plainly.
- **A spectrum.** Rich imagination → immersive daydreaming → *maladaptive* only when
  it causes real impairment/distress. Most vivid daydreamers are totally fine.
- **MD is a *proposed* condition**, not yet in the DSM; often co-occurs with ADHD,
  OCD, anxiety, depression. Measured with the **MDS-16**.
- Often begins in **childhood**.

## On-screen numbers (StatCards)
- Studies of people with MD report spending, on average, a large share of waking
  time daydreaming — some **several hours a day** (framed as "hours", not a precise
  universal figure).

## Precision caveats
- Prevalence of MD is **uncertain** (no DSM status) — no hard stat claimed.
- Traits are a **spectrum/tendencies**, not a diagnosis; vivid imagination is common
  and healthy.

## Disclaimer (also in metadata)
Education and curiosity, not diagnosis. A vivid inner world is usually a gift, not a
disorder — daydreamers know it's imaginary. But if daydreaming feels compulsive and
is harming your sleep, work, or relationships, that's "maladaptive daydreaming," and
a therapist (ideally familiar with it) can genuinely help.
