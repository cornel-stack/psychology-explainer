# SOURCES — "Every Feeling You Can't Name, Explained"

Every factual claim in the script traces to an entry here. Coined terms from John
Koenig's **Dictionary of Obscure Sorrows** (web project begun 2009; book, Simon &
Schuster, 2021) are credited to him on-screen and in the description.

Legend: **[RESEARCH]** = has a psychology study / named researcher / mechanism
(≥8 required; we have 9).

---

### 1. L'appel du vide / "call of the void" (High Place Phenomenon) **[RESEARCH]**
- Meaning: the brief urge to jump felt in a high place, in people with **no** wish to die. French: *the call of the void* (*appel* = call, *vide* = void).
- Source: **Hames, Ribeiro, Smith & Joiner (2012), "An urge to jump affirms the urge to live," *Journal of Affective Disorders* 136(3):1114–1120. Florida State University.**
- On-screen anchor: surveyed **431 students**; about **half** of those who had *never* been suicidal had still felt the urge. Theory: a fast fear "step back!" signal that the slower conscious mind misreads as "I want to jump." (We frame it as a theory.)

### 2. Sonder (coined) 
- Meaning: the realization that every passerby has a life as vivid as your own.
- Source: **John Koenig, *The Dictionary of Obscure Sorrows*** (coinage, ~2012). Built from German *sonder* ("apart") + French *sonder* ("to fathom"). Neologism, not a historical word — credited to Koenig.

### 3. Frisson / musical chills **[RESEARCH]**
- Meaning: the shiver/goosebumps at a musical peak ("skin orgasm"). French *frisson* = "shiver."
- Sources: **Blood & Zatorre (2001), *PNAS* 98(20):11818–11823, McGill** — PET scans show chills light up the brain's **reward circuitry** (ventral striatum, midbrain, amygdala, OFC). **Salimpoor et al. (2011), *Nature Neuroscience*, McGill** — directly measured **dopamine** release during chills (raclopride PET).
- Prevalence stat: **≈2 in 3 people** report music-induced chills. Source: **Panksepp (1995), "The emotional sources of 'chills' induced by music," *Music Perception* 13(2):171–207** (reported ~70–90% depending on sample/measure; we state "about two-thirds" conservatively).
- On-screen anchor: same reward chemistry as food, sex, and drugs; **dopamine** attributed to the 2011 study.

### 4. Tip-of-the-tongue (+ presque vu) **[RESEARCH]**
- Meaning: certainty you know a word you can't retrieve. *Presque vu* (French, "almost seen") = the sibling sense of a near-epiphany.
- Source: **Brown & McNeill (1966), "The 'tip of the tongue' phenomenon," *J. Verbal Learning & Verbal Behavior* 5(4):325–337. Harvard.**
- On-screen anchor: they induced it by reading **definitions of rare words**; people in the state could guess the **first letter** and **syllable count** above chance — partial memory access during the block. Stat: in the original study participants named the correct **first letter about 57% of the time** when stuck (well above chance). (Brown & McNeill 1966.)

### 5. Jamais vu **[RESEARCH]**
- Meaning: the opposite of déjà vu — the familiar suddenly feels alien. French *jamais vu* = "never seen."
- Source: **Moulin, Bell, Turunen, Baharin & O'Connor (2021), "...induction of jamais vu in the laboratory: word alienation and semantic satiation," *Memory* 29(7):933–942. Université Grenoble Alpes & University of St Andrews. Ig Nobel Prize for Literature, 2023.**
- On-screen anchor: repeat one word and it starts to feel strange/meaningless after **~1 minute (~33 repetitions)** — "semantic satiation."

### 6. Saudade (+ nostalgia research) **[RESEARCH]**
- Meaning: bittersweet longing for an absent person/time. **Portuguese/Galician**; from Latin *solitas* ("solitude"). **Pronunciation locked: European Portuguese "sow-DAH-duh"** (used consistently).
- Nostalgia research: **Sedikides & Wildschut, University of Southampton** (e.g., "Finding Meaning in Nostalgia," *Review of General Psychology*, 2018).
- On-screen anchor: experiments show nostalgia **raises mood, self-esteem, social connectedness and sense of meaning** — it's psychologically useful, not just sad.

### 7. Anemoia (coined)
- Meaning: nostalgia for a time you never knew. **John Koenig, *Dictionary of Obscure Sorrows*.** Greek root *ánemos* = "wind" (real root; assembled word is Koenig's).

### 8. Gigil / cute aggression **[RESEARCH]**
- Meaning: the urge to squeeze something unbearably cute. **Gigil** = Tagalog (added to the **OED in 2025**).
- Sources: **Aragón, Clark, Dyer & Bargh (2015), "Dimorphous expressions of positive emotion," *Psychological Science* 26(3):259–273. Yale.** **Stavropoulos & Alba (2018), "It's so Cute I Could Crush It!", *Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience* 12:300. UC Riverside.**
- On-screen anchor: **EEG** study — cute aggression engages **both** the reward and emotion systems; theorized as a **"dimorphous" regulator** that tempers an overwhelming surge of positive feeling.

### 9. Torschlusspanik (real German word)
- Meaning: the anxiety that time's running out and doors are closing (career, relationships, kids). Literal: *Tor* (gate) + *Schluss* (closing) + *Panik* = **"gate-shut panic."**
- On-screen anchor: medieval origin — rushing back inside the **city gates before they shut at nightfall**.

### 10. Kenopsia (coined)
- Meaning: the eerie atmosphere of a place usually busy but now empty. **John Koenig, *Dictionary of Obscure Sorrows*.** Greek *kenós* (empty) + *-opsia* (seeing) = "seeing emptiness."

### 11. Collective effervescence **[RESEARCH]**
- Meaning: the electric unity of a crowd moving/feeling as one.
- Sources: **Émile Durkheim (1912), *The Elementary Forms of Religious Life*.** Modern: **Gabriel, Valenti, Naragon-Gainey & Young (2017), *Psychological Assessment* 29(12):1349–1362. University at Buffalo** — the **TEAM** scale.
- On-screen anchor: people drawn to collective assembly report **less loneliness and more meaning in life**.

### 12. Amae (real Japanese concept) **[RESEARCH]**
- Meaning: the sweet security of leaning on someone's goodwill; being able to be a little selfish and still be cared for. 甘え, related to *amai* ("sweet").
- Source: **Takeo Doi (1971), *The Anatomy of Dependence* (Amae no Kōzō).** Psychiatrist, University of Tokyo.
- On-screen anchor: Doi argued the feeling is universal, but Japanese is unusual in having a whole **vocabulary** built around it.

### 13. Awumbuk
- Meaning: the heavy emptiness after guests leave. **Baining people, Papua New Guinea**; ethnographer **Jane Fajans (Cornell)**; popularized by **Tiffany Watt Smith, *The Book of Human Emotions* (2015)**.
- On-screen anchor (attribute vivid framing to Watt Smith): the Baining leave out a **bowl of water overnight** to absorb the lingering heaviness, then throw it out at dawn.

### 14. Fernweh (real German word)
- Meaning: an ache to be somewhere far away. Literal: *fern* (far) + *Weh* (ache) = **"farsickness"** — the mirror of **Heimweh** (homesickness).

### 15. Elevation / moral elevation **[RESEARCH]**
- Meaning: the warm, chest-swelling uplift from witnessing someone's goodness.
- Source: **Jonathan Haidt (2000, 2003), University of Virginia; Algoe & Haidt (2009), *J. Positive Psychology* 4(2):105–127.**
- On-screen anchor: felt as **warmth/openness in the chest**, sometimes tears; Haidt links it to the **vagus nerve** and a motivation to do good yourself. (Oxytocin link is speculative — not stated as fact.)
