## Description
Here's an uncomfortable statistic: about 95% of people believe they're self-aware — but
when researchers measure it, only around 10–15% actually are. Self-awareness isn't one
thing you either have or don't. It's a ladder, with many levels, and most of us are
standing lower on it than we'd like to believe. The good news: it's a skill, and you
can climb.

A walk up the ladder, rung by rung:

0:00 The self-awareness paradox
Level 0 the blind spot (Dunning–Kruger) · level 1 hindsight · level 2 in the moment ·
level 3 your inner world · level 4 the harder mirror (how others see you + the Johari
window) · level 5 watching the mind (metacognition) · level 6 the observer (you are not
your thoughts) · the introspection trap (ask "what", not "why") · and the top:
clarity + self-acceptance.

🪞 Real self-awareness isn't ruthless self-criticism — it's seeing yourself clearly AND
holding it kindly, because you can only face honestly what you won't punish. To climb:
seek honest feedback, ask "what" not "why", practise noticing your mind, hold your
self-story loosely, and pair it all with self-compassion.

⚠️ It's a lifelong practice, not a destination — even the most aware people have blind
spots (that humility is itself a sign of it). Not a diagnosis.
