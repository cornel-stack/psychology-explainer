## Description
Try to remember being two — your first steps, your first word, the feeling of being
carried. You can't. Just a blank. And here's the strange part: you spent those years
doing the most important learning of your life, and you remember none of it.

This is the wondrous science of childhood amnesia:

0:00 The blank before age three
The great forgetting · you did remember, then (babies form memories too) · a
half-built memory-binder (the hippocampus) · growth erases (new neurons overwrite
old memories) · no words, no story · there was no "me" yet · your first memory is
probably fake (built from photos) · shaped anyway (implicit memory) · and the
reminiscing effect.

🧠 You can't recall those years, but you're made of them — your brain grew so fast
and so beautifully that it had to forget, in order to become you. (This is also why
very early adversity isn't consciously remembered yet can leave a mark — and why a
good therapist can help, even when you can't recall.)
