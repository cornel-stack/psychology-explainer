# SOURCES — "Every Sleep Glitch You Might Experience"

Channel: **Dear Brain**. Framing: sleep isn't an on/off switch — it's a staged
process (NREM light → NREM deep → REM, ~90-min cycles), and most "glitches" are the
machinery of **falling asleep, dreaming, and waking** briefly misfiring at the
borderland. Nearly all are **common and benign**; a few warrant a doctor. All art
is bespoke (the surreal sleep bestiary). Mostly classified per the **AASM
International Classification of Sleep Disorders** (parasomnias).

## The 15 glitches
| # | Glitch | What it is | Stage / note | Source |
|---|--------|-----------|--------------|--------|
| 1 | **Hypnic jerk** (sleep start) | A sudden jolt / falling sensation as you drift off. | Sleep onset; benign; ~60–70% of people. | Common; hypnagogic myoclonus. |
| 2 | **Sleep paralysis** | Awake but unable to move, often with a "presence". | REM atonia intrudes into wakefulness; ~**7.6%** lifetime. | Sharpless & Barber (2011) meta-analysis. |
| 3 | **Hypnagogic hallucinations** | Vivid images/sounds at sleep onset (hypnopompic on waking). | Borderland of wake/sleep; very common. | Sleep-medicine texts. |
| 4 | **Exploding head syndrome** | A loud bang/crash in your head as you fall asleep; painless. | Sensory parasomnia; benign. | Sharpless (2015). |
| 5 | **False awakening** | Dreaming you woke up (sometimes nested). | Often with lucidity / sleep paralysis. | Dream research. |
| 6 | **Lucid dreaming** | Knowing you're dreaming, sometimes steering it. | REM; prefrontal activation; eye-signal verified. | LaBerge (Stanford). |
| 7 | **Sleepwalking** (somnambulism) | Walking/acting while asleep, no memory. | NREM deep-sleep parasomnia; commoner in kids. | AASM. |
| 8 | **Sleep talking** (somniloquy) | Talking in your sleep; usually gibberish. | NREM or REM; benign. | AASM. |
| 9 | **Night terrors** | Screaming/panic in deep sleep, no memory — ≠ nightmare. | NREM, early night; vs nightmare (REM, late, remembered). | AASM. |
| 10 | **The Tetris effect** | Dreaming of a repetitive daytime activity. | Memory consolidation / dream incorporation. | Stickgold et al. (2000). |
| 11 | **Sleep inertia** | Grogginess/fog for ~15–30 min on waking. | Worse waking from deep sleep. | Sleep-medicine texts. |
| 12 | **Microsleep** | Brief (seconds) involuntary sleep while awake. | Sleep-deprivation sign; dangerous while driving. | Vigilance research. |
| 13 | **REM behaviour disorder** | Acting out dreams (atonia fails). | Can be an early sign of neurodegeneration — see a doctor. | Schenck & Mahowald. |
| 14 | **Déjà rêvé** | "I've dreamed this before." | Memory phenomenon (cousin of déjà vu). | Memory research. |
| 15 | **Recurring / chase dreams** | The same themes (chased, falling, teeth, exams). | Common; not literal prophecy. | Dream-theme surveys. |

## On-screen numbers (StatCards)
- **~1/3** of life is spent asleep (anchor).
- **~70%** experience hypnic jerks.
- **~7.6% (≈8%)** lifetime prevalence of sleep paralysis (Sharpless & Barber 2011).
- **~90 min** per sleep cycle; ~4–6 cycles a night.

## Precision caveats (handled in VO)
- "Glitches" is a friendly word — most are **normal variants**, not disorders.
- The sleep-paralysis "intruder" is a **REM-dream intrusion**, not a demon — debunked
  gently.
- **Microsleep** safety: never drive drowsy (framed as a real danger, not a quirk).
- **REM behaviour disorder** can signal later neurological conditions — flagged as a
  "see a professional", **not** diagnosed here.
- Prevalence figures are approximate and vary by study/sample.

## Disclaimer (also in metadata)
Education and curiosity, not diagnosis. Most sleep glitches are common and harmless;
but loud snoring with gasping, acting out dreams, frequent terrors, or dangerous
daytime sleepiness deserve a doctor. If sleep is seriously affecting your life, talk
to a professional.
