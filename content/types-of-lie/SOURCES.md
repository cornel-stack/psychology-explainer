# Sources — Every Type of Lie Explained

Why everyone lies, the distinct kinds of lie (from harmless to corrosive), what each one
costs, and why the most dangerous lies are the ones we tell ourselves. Honest, warm,
evidence-informed. Not a license to lie — a map of a universal human behaviour.

## Everyone lies — the numbers
- Lying is **universal and frequent.** Classic diary studies (**Bella DePaulo**) found
  most people tell **one to two lies a day**, usually small ones. Later work (**Serota
  & Levine**) adds nuance: lying is **unevenly distributed** — **most people lie
  rarely**, while a **small number of prolific liars** tell the majority of lies. So
  "everyone lies" is true, but a few people do a lot of the heavy lifting.

## Why we lie
- Most lies are **not malicious.** We lie to **protect ourselves** (avoid punishment or
  embarrassment), to **protect others' feelings**, to **smooth social situations**, to
  **gain an advantage**, and to **manage the impression** we make. The motive matters
  enormously for whether a lie is trivial or toxic.

## Lying is cognitively expensive
- Telling the truth is the brain's **default**; lying takes **extra mental effort** —
  you must suppress the truth, invent an alternative, keep it consistent, and track who
  knows what. That's why big or sustained lies are **hard to maintain** (and why
  liars often leak stress cues — though detection is genuinely unreliable).

## The main types (stated in the video)
- **White lie** — small, kind, socially lubricating ("I love it!"). Usually harmless,
  arguably prosocial; **total honesty** would make society brutal.
- **Lie of omission** — misleading by **leaving things out** rather than stating a
  falsehood. Technically "didn't lie," but intends to deceive.
- **Exaggeration** — stretching the truth to impress or emphasise.
- **Bald-faced lie** — a bold, knowing falsehood told even when the truth is obvious.
- **The cover-up (the snowball)** — one lie that **requires more lies** to sustain,
  compounding until it collapses ("oh what a tangled web…").
- **The bluff** — pretending to have a capability, intention, or hand you don't (poker,
  negotiation); deception by **performance**.
- **The noble / altruistic lie** — told to **protect** someone (a surprise party, a
  kindness to the dying). Well-intentioned, ethically debated.
- **Compulsive / pathological lying** — lying **habitually, often without clear benefit**;
  can be a symptom of deeper difficulty, not simple dishonesty.
- **The broken promise** — a **future-tense lie**: words of intention never meant, or
  not kept.
- **Self-deception** — the lies we tell **ourselves** (rationalising, denial, motivated
  reasoning). Often the **most damaging**, because there's no one to catch us.

## Lying is a developmental milestone
- Children typically start lying around **age 3–4** — and it's actually a sign of
  **healthy cognitive development**: it requires **theory of mind** (knowing others hold
  different beliefs) plus impulse control. The ability to lie is built into a normal
  mind.

## White lies vs. radical honesty
- Small prosocial lies are the **grease of social life**; a world of total, blunt honesty
  would be cruel and unworkable. But chronic or self-serving lying **erodes trust** — and
  trust, once broken, is slow and costly to rebuild. The healthy line runs between
  **kindness** and **deception for gain**.

## Honest takeaways (stated in the video)
- Notice **why** you're lying — kindness, or self-protection and advantage?
- Watch the **cover-up snowball**; the second lie is usually worse than the first.
- **Keep your promises** — the future-tense lie quietly corrodes trust.
- And above all, **catch your own self-deceptions** — the lies you believe are the ones
  that run your life. **Not a diagnosis**; compulsive lying that damages your life is
  worth talking through with a professional.
