# Sources — The Psychology of Motivation

Why waiting to "feel motivated" backfires, what actually drives us, the difference
between the fire that lasts and the one that fizzles, and how to get (and stay) going.
Practical, evidence-based, kind. Not a diagnosis.

## The big myth: waiting to feel motivated
- We treat motivation as a feeling we must **wait for** before acting. But research and
  experience point the other way: **action usually comes first, and motivation
  follows.** Starting — even tiny — creates momentum, progress, and the feeling we were
  waiting for. Waiting to feel ready is the single most common motivation trap.

## Intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation
- **Deci & Ryan's Self-Determination Theory:** **intrinsic** motivation (doing something
  for its own interest, enjoyment, or meaning) is far more **durable** than **extrinsic**
  motivation (rewards, money, grades, praise, pressure). The **overjustification
  effect**: paying people to do something they already loved can actually **reduce**
  their intrinsic drive — the reward replaces the joy.

## The three fuels (Self-Determination Theory)
- Intrinsic motivation thrives when three basic needs are met:
  - **Autonomy** — I **chose** this; it's mine, not imposed.
  - **Competence** — I'm **making progress**, getting better, seeing it work.
  - **Relatedness** — it **connects me** to others / something bigger.
- Meet these three, and motivation tends to flow on its own.

## Dopamine is about the chase, not the prize
- Dopamine is often mislabelled the "pleasure chemical." It's really the **wanting /
  seeking / anticipation** chemical — it drives the **pursuit** of a reward, the "go get
  it." It spikes for **anticipation and progress**, which is why **getting started and
  seeing movement** feels so motivating, and why vague, distant goals feel so flat.

## The progress principle — small wins
- **Teresa Amabile's research:** of all the things that boost motivation day to day, the
  single most powerful is **making progress in meaningful work.** Small wins compound:
  visible progress feeds the drive to keep going. **Momentum**, not willpower, is the
  real engine.

## Willpower is limited; systems win
- Relying on **discipline / willpower** alone tends to fail — it's a finite resource and
  a bad long-term strategy. **Habits, systems, and environment design** (James Clear and
  others) beat willpower: make the good thing **easy** and the distraction **hard**
  (reduce friction), and you need far less motivation to act.

## Why motivation dies
- Goals that are **too big, too vague, or lack a clear next step** freeze us.
- **Temporal discounting:** we heavily **discount future rewards**, so motivation is weak
  when the payoff is far away — bring the reward (or the progress) **closer and sooner.**
- **Fear and perfectionism** disguise themselves as "not feeling motivated."
- A task with **no "why"** — no felt meaning — rarely moves us.

## How to get (and stay) motivated (stated in the video)
- **Act first** — don't wait to feel it; start, and let motivation catch up.
- **Start absurdly small** — the **two-minute rule**; lower the bar until starting is
  almost effortless. Done beats perfect.
- **Make it intrinsic** — find the genuinely interesting or meaningful angle; connect it
  to your **why**.
- **Reduce friction / design your environment** — make the good action the easy one.
- **Chase small wins** — track progress; momentum is self-feeding.
- **Be kind to yourself** — **self-compassion** motivates better than self-criticism;
  shame drains drive, it doesn't build it.

## Honest caveat
- A **persistent, total** loss of motivation — where nothing feels worth doing — isn't
  laziness and isn't fixed by a productivity hack. It can be a real sign of **depression
  or burnout** (avolition / anhedonia). **Not a diagnosis.** If that's you, please be
  gentle and reach out for support — that flatness is a symptom, not a character flaw.
