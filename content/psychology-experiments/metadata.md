## Description
Would you deliver a deadly electric shock to a stranger, just because a man in a lab
coat told you to? You'd say no. And yet, when psychologists actually tested it, around
two thirds of ordinary people did exactly that. The experiments that built psychology
didn't just study the mind — they held up a mirror, and what we saw was deeply
unsettling.

A tour of the landmark studies, one by one:

0:00 Would you obey?
Pavlov's dogs (classical conditioning) · Little Albert (a conditioned fear) · Milgram's
obedience · Asch conformity · the Stanford Prison (held loosely — heavily critiqued) ·
the bystander effect · the Bobo doll (learning by watching) · Harlow's monkeys · the
marshmallow test (with the modern twist) · the invisible gorilla · and what they all
taught us.

🧠 The humbling thread: our behaviour is shaped far more by the *situation* than by the
person we think we are. Several of these studies caused real harm and could never be
run today — they're precisely why modern research ethics (informed consent, debriefing,
review boards) exist. Knowing them is a quiet armour: it helps you notice when you're
conforming, obeying, or looking away — so you can choose differently.

⚠️ Presented with honesty about the ethical problems and the replication nuances — not
an endorsement of the methods. Not a diagnosis; a tour of ideas, held with humility.

Welcome to Dear Brain — calm, illustrated psychology, one idea at a time.
