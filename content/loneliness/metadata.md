## Description
You can be in a packed room, laughing along, and feel completely, achingly alone. Or
scrolling hundreds of friends on a screen — more connected than any humans in history,
and emptier than ever. Loneliness is one of the most painful, misunderstood feelings
there is.

This is the compassionate, hopeful science of loneliness:

0:00 Alone in a crowd
It's about the gap (quality, not quantity) · a signal, not a flaw (like hunger) ·
alone isn't lonely (solitude can be lovely) · it hurts your body (as serious as
smoking) · the loneliness trap (it feeds itself) · the tinted lens (it distorts what
you see) · two kinds of lonely (emotional vs social) · breaking the cycle · and the
way back: give, and open up.

💛 That ache isn't a sign you're unlovable — it's a sign you're human, built for
connection, and currently missing some. The connection you're aching for is one
small, brave, open hand away. (If loneliness is heavy and constant, especially with
low mood, that can travel with depression — reaching out for support is brave, not
weak.)

Welcome to Dear Brain — calm, illustrated psychology, one idea at a time.
