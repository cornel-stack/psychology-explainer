# Sources — The Psychology of Habits

How habits actually work, and how to build good ones (and break bad ones). Core
science from behavioural psychology and neuroscience, popularised by Charles
Duhigg (*The Power of Habit*) and James Clear (*Atomic Habits*).

## Key ideas
- **~40% of daily behaviour is habit**, not conscious decision (Wood, Quinn &
  Kashy, 2002 — roughly 40–45% of actions are repeated in the same context).
  Much of life runs on autopilot.
- **Why the brain forms habits:** automating repeated behaviours saves mental
  energy; the basal ganglia store these routines so the thinking brain (prefrontal
  cortex) can switch off. Habits become literal **worn neural pathways** —
  "grooves" that get deeper with repetition.
- **The habit loop (Duhigg):** **Cue → Routine → Reward.** A trigger prompts a
  behaviour that delivers a reward; repeat, and it automates.
- **Craving is the real engine:** the brain starts to *anticipate* the reward when
  it senses the cue; that craving (dopamine-driven anticipation) is what pulls the
  routine out of you. (Dopamine spikes at the cue, not just the reward.)
- **Why bad habits stick, good ones are hard:** rewards that are **immediate** get
  reinforced; costs/benefits that are **delayed** don't. Bad habits feel good now
  and hurt later; good habits cost now and pay off later — so we chase the instant
  reward. (Temporal discounting.)
- **You can't erase a habit, only replace it (the Golden Rule, Duhigg):** keep the
  same **cue** and **reward**, swap the **routine** in the middle.

## The Four Laws (Clear) — to build a good habit
1. **Make it obvious** — design your environment/cues (cue is the trigger).
2. **Make it attractive** — pair it with something you want (temptation bundling).
3. **Make it easy** — reduce friction; the **two-minute rule**; tiny habits (BJ
   Fogg). (Inverting each law breaks a bad habit: invisible, unattractive, hard,
   unsatisfying.)
4. **Make it satisfying** — add an immediate reward; **habit tracking** (the
   satisfaction of marking it done).

## Scaling it
- **Habit stacking (Clear / Fogg's "anchoring"):** "After I [current habit], I
  will [new habit]" — attach the new one to an existing routine as its cue.
- **Identity-based habits:** the deepest change is becoming "the kind of person
  who…"; each small action is a vote for that identity. Outcome-based goals fade;
  identity-based habits stick.
- **The compound effect / aggregation of marginal gains:** 1% better every day
  compounds enormously over a year; tiny habits, repeated, are the real engine of
  change. Progress is often invisible until it crosses a threshold ("plateau of
  latent potential").

## Honest framing (stated in the video)
- Habit change is a **skill and a system, not a matter of willpower** — if you've
  failed before, you weren't weak; you were relying on motivation instead of
  design. Be kind to yourself; expect slips; focus on getting back on track fast,
  not on perfection.
- Psycho-education, not therapy; genuinely compulsive behaviours or addictions may
  need professional support.
