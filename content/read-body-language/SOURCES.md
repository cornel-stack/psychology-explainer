# Sources — How to Read Body Language

What the body actually says, how to read it honestly (clusters and baselines, not
single "tells"), the most reliable signals, and why it's a skill of probabilities, not
a lie detector. Evidence-based, practical, humble. Not a diagnosis, not mind-reading.

## Nonverbals carry real weight — but mind the myth
- Most of the emotional and relational meaning in a face-to-face exchange is carried
  **nonverbally** — tone, face, posture, gesture. BUT the famous "**93% of
  communication is nonverbal**" (Mehrabian's 7-38-55) is **widely misquoted**: it
  applied narrowly to messages about **feelings and attitudes** when words and tone
  conflict. We state this honestly in the video. Nonverbals matter enormously for
  *emotion*; they don't replace words for *content*.

## Read clusters, not single cues
- The cardinal rule (Joe Navarro, former FBI behavioural analyst): **never read a
  single gesture in isolation.** Crossed arms might mean defensiveness — or a cold
  room. Meaning comes from **clusters** of several cues pointing the same way, read in
  **context**.

## Establish a baseline first
- Before you can spot a meaningful signal, you need the person's **normal** — their
  baseline of fidgeting, eye contact, gesture. What matters is the **deviation** from
  that baseline when a topic changes, not the absolute behaviour.

## Universal expressions and micro-expressions
- **Paul Ekman:** a set of **basic facial expressions** (happiness, sadness, fear,
  anger, surprise, disgust, contempt) are recognised **across cultures**.
  **Micro-expressions** — involuntary flashes lasting as little as **1/25 of a second**
  — can briefly **leak** a concealed emotion before the social mask returns.

## The honest feet (and the limbic leak)
- Navarro's insight: the parts of the body furthest from the brain's conscious control
  are the **most honest**. The **feet and legs** react first and most truthfully
  (freeze-flight-fight), the **face** is the most practised and controlled — and so the
  **least** reliable. **Feet tend to point where a person wants to go.**

## Pacifying / self-soothing behaviours
- Under stress, people **self-soothe**: touching the neck, rubbing hands, stroking the
  arm, playing with a collar. These **pacifying behaviours** signal that something just
  raised discomfort — a useful *deviation* cue (again: cluster + context, not proof).

## Eye contact and personal space
- **Eye contact** has a comfortable middle: too little reads as evasive or anxious, too
  much as aggressive; relaxed, intermittent gaze signals connection. **Proxemics**
  (Edward Hall): we carry invisible **space zones** — intimate, personal, social,
  public — and discomfort when they're crossed is itself a signal.

## Mirroring and ventral fronting
- **Mirroring (isopraxism):** when rapport is genuine, people **unconsciously echo**
  each other's posture and gestures. **Ventral fronting** — pointing the torso and
  front of the body toward someone — signals comfort and liking; **ventral denial**
  (angling away, a barrier arm, an object held across the body) signals the opposite.

## The genuine smile
- A **Duchenne smile** (genuine) engages the muscles around the **eyes**, creating
  crow's-feet; a merely social smile uses the mouth alone. The eyes are the giveaway.

## Honest caveats (stated in the video)
- Body language is **probabilistic, not a lie detector.** No single cue has one fixed
  meaning; people are only about **54% accurate** at detecting lies from behaviour.
  Cultural and neurodivergent differences are real (eye contact norms vary; autistic
  body language is different, not dishonest). Use this to **understand and connect**,
  to notice your own signals and others' comfort — **never to accuse** or to "catch"
  people. Not a diagnosis.
