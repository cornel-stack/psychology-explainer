# SOURCES — "Why Your Brain Fears 3 AM"

Channel: **Dear Brain**. Thesis: 3 a.m. dread isn't a message — it's **biology**.
At the small hours your body hits its circadian low, cortisol is rising, the
rational front-brain is offline while the fear centre runs free, and there's nothing
to distract you. The same worry that's a molehill at 9 a.m. becomes a mountain at 3.
All art bespoke.

## Why it happens (the chapters)
| Factor | What's going on | Source |
|--------|-----------------|--------|
| **The circadian low** | Core body temperature and alertness bottom out around **3–5 a.m.** — your body clock's nadir. | Circadian rhythm / SCN; core-temp nadir research. |
| **The cortisol surge** | Cortisol starts climbing in the **last hours of sleep** to prepare you to wake (the cortisol awakening response). Awake with nothing to do, it reads as anxiety. | Cortisol awakening response literature. |
| **Rational brain offline** | Sleep loss and the sleep state leave the **prefrontal cortex** under-active while the **amygdala** (fear) is *hyper*-reactive — alarm with no brakes. | Yoo, Walker et al. (2007), amygdala–prefrontal decoupling. |
| **Melatonin high / body coldest** | You're chemically primed for sleep, not problem-solving; thinking is foggy and bleak. | Sleep-medicine texts. |
| **No distractions** | No work, people, or screens to drown the mind — rumination gets the whole stage. | Rumination research (Nolen-Hoeksema). |
| **Loneliness** | Feeling like the only person awake in the world amplifies dread. | — |
| **Catastrophizing** | Low mood + no perspective → molehills become mountains; problems feel unsolvable. | Cognitive distortion / catastrophizing. |
| **Ancient vigilance** | Darkness meant predators; the brain is evolutionarily primed to be watchful and afraid at night ("the hour of the wolf"). | Evolutionary/threat-vigilance accounts. |

## What to do (the payoff chapter)
- **Don't problem-solve at 3 a.m.** — the brain is lying; wait for daylight.
- Get up briefly, keep lights **dim**, do something calm/boring; don't clock-watch.
- **Write the worry down** ("park it for the morning").
- Name it: *"this is just the hour, not the truth."* By 9 a.m. it shrinks.

## On-screen numbers (StatCards)

## Precision caveats (handled in VO)
- Waking in the night is **normal** (historically people slept biphasically); it's the
  *spiralling*, not the waking, that's the problem.
- This is about everyday 3 a.m. dread — **persistent** insomnia, panic, or anxiety
  deserve a professional (stated).
- Mechanisms are simplified; individual rhythms vary.

## Disclaimer (also in metadata)
Education and curiosity, not medical advice. Occasional 3 a.m. dread is normal human
biology. But if sleeplessness, panic, or night-time anxiety are a regular, serious
problem, please talk to a doctor — this is a pattern a professional can really help with.
