# SOURCES — "Every Level of IQ Explained"

Channel: **Dear Brain**. Framing: IQ is a **score on a standardized reasoning
test**, not a measure of a person's worth, character, or potential. Modern tests
(**Wechsler** WAIS/WISC; **Stanford-Binet**) use a **deviation IQ**: scores are
scaled to a **mean of 100** and a **standard deviation of 15**, and the population
falls on a **bell curve** (normal distribution). The first practical test came from
**Alfred Binet & Théodore Simon** (1905) — built to find French schoolchildren who
needed *extra help*, explicitly **not** to rank human worth (Binet warned against
treating it as fixed). The term "IQ" traces to **William Stern**; **Lewis Terman**
brought the Stanford-Binet to the US.

## The levels (Wechsler classification; ~ of population)
| Band | Label (video) | Wechsler class | ~ population | Notes |
|------|---------------|----------------|-------------|-------|
| ~70 | Below average | Borderline / Extremely Low | bottom ~2–9% | A score alone ≠ "intellectual disability" (ID also requires adaptive-functioning limits). Thrives with concrete, hands-on, well-supported tasks. |
| ~85 | Low average | Low Average | ~16% | Handles daily life; abstract/academic work is more effortful. |
| 100 | Average | Average (90–109) | **~50%** | The statistical middle — most people. |
| 115 | Above average | High Average | ~16% | +1 SD; learns quickly, does well in complex work/school. |
| 130 | Gifted | Very Superior | **top ~2%** | +2 SD; ~Mensa cutoff (98th percentile). Gifted ≠ automatically happy or successful. |
| 145 | Highly gifted | (very superior tail) | ~top 0.1% (≈1 in 1,000) | +3 SD; rare; can feel socially out of step. |
| 160+ | "Genius" (colloquial) | (extreme tail) | rarer than ~1 in 30,000 | +4 SD; "genius" is a colloquial label, not a clinical cutoff. |

## On-screen numbers (StatCards)
- **~50%** cluster around average (90–109).
- **~68 / 95%** within 1 / 2 SD (bell-curve fact; used verbally).
- **top ~2%** at 130+ (Mensa/"gifted").
- **≈1 in 30,000** at ~160 (framed as "rarer than").
- **+3 points per decade** — the **Flynn effect** (James Flynn): average raw scores
  rose through the 20th century, so tests are periodically **re-normed**; shows IQ
  is not a fixed, timeless quantity.

## Myth-busting anchors (handled in VO)
- **Gifted ≠ destiny.** Terman's longitudinal "Termites" (high-IQ kids) mostly did
  well, but outcomes tracked **drive, opportunity and environment**, not IQ alone;
  famously, two boys he screened **out** for low scores — **William Shockley** and
  **Luis Alvarez** — later won **Nobel Prizes**. (Widely cited; exact screening
  details debated — presented as illustrative, not precise biography.)
- **IQ predicts *some* things** (moderate correlation with school and complex-job
  performance) but **not** creativity, emotional intelligence, wisdom, character,
  or worth.
- **Scores move:** affected by education, health, nutrition, practice, test
  conditions — a snapshot, not a soul-reading.

## Precision caveats
- Bands are **fuzzy ranges**, not hard lines; different tests classify slightly
  differently.
- "**Genius**" at 160 is colloquial; **intellectual disability** is a clinical
  diagnosis (score **plus** adaptive deficits), never a bare number.
- Percentages are **approximate** and test-dependent.
- Prevalence/"1 in N" figures are rounded bell-curve estimates.

## Disclaimer (also in metadata)
Education and curiosity, not an IQ test or a diagnosis. IQ measures a narrow slice
of thinking under test conditions — it is **not** a measure of your worth,
character, creativity, or what you can become. Be kind about a number that was
never meant to rank people.
