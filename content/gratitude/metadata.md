## Description
You could have a warm bed, food in the fridge, people who love you, a working body —
and still spend the whole day feeling like it's somehow not enough. We're surrounded by
good things we've completely stopped seeing. Gratitude is simply the skill of learning
to see them again.

This is the real science of gratitude:

0:00 Surrounded by invisible good
What it really is (a skill, not a mood) · the negativity bias (bad shouts, good
whispers) · hedonic adaptation (the good fades to normal) · scarcity vs abundance (same
life, different lens) · what it does to you (the proven effects) · it binds us (the glue
of connection) · four ways to practise · and the honest line vs toxic positivity.

🙏 Gratitude doesn't give you a better life — it helps you notice the one you already
have. Keep a *specific* gratitude journal, savour good moments, write the thank-you
letter, and try mental subtraction (imagine it gone). Your life right now is quietly
full of things someone else is praying for.

⚠️ Real gratitude isn't toxic positivity — it sits *beside* grief and struggle, never
denies them, and is never a substitute for real support. Not a diagnosis; if low mood
persists, please reach out.

Welcome to Dear Brain — calm, illustrated psychology, one idea at a time.
