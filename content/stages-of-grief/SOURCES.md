# Sources — Every Stage of Grief Explained

A compassionate guide to grief, built on the famous five stages but corrected with
the modern understanding of how loss actually works. Handled with care: grief is
universal, deeply personal, and this is understanding, not a prescription.

## The five stages (Kübler-Ross)
From Elisabeth Kübler-Ross, *On Death and Dying* (1969): **Denial, Anger,
Bargaining, Depression, Acceptance** (DABDA).
- **Denial** — shock, numbness, "this can't be happening"; a buffer that lets
  reality in slowly.
- **Anger** — "why me? it's not fair"; anger at the situation, others, the self,
  the person lost. A natural, necessary stage; strength returning.
- **Bargaining** — "if only… / what if…"; the mind trying to regain control, often
  with guilt and endless replays.
- **Depression** — the deep sadness, emptiness and withdrawal; not a disorder here
  but an appropriate response to loss.
- **Acceptance** — not "being okay" with the loss, but coming to terms with the new
  reality and learning to live with it.

## The crucial correction (stated clearly in the video)
- **The stages are NOT linear, not a fixed sequence, and not mandatory.** Even
  Kübler-Ross said they were never meant to be a tidy checklist, and she originally
  described them for *dying patients*, not the bereaved. People skip stages,
  revisit them, feel several at once, or none. **Grief has no timeline.**
- **Grief is not only for death.** We grieve breakups, lost jobs, lost health, the
  end of a chapter, a life that didn't happen. Any significant loss can trigger it.
- **"Grief is love with nowhere to go."** (Jamie Anderson.) The depth of grief
  reflects the depth of love.

## The modern model
- **The sixth stage — Meaning** (David Kessler, *Finding Meaning*, 2019): after
  acceptance, many find a way to make meaning from the loss, which honours the
  person/thing lost. Not required, but often where healing deepens.
- **Grief comes in waves** (not stages): intense at first, then the waves come
  further apart over time, though they can return unexpectedly (triggers,
  anniversaries). (The "waves of grief" model.)
- **You don't "get over" grief — you grow around it** (Lois Tonkin's model): the
  grief doesn't necessarily shrink; your life grows larger around it, so it takes
  up proportionally less space over time.
- **The "ball in the box" metaphor:** grief is a ball bouncing in a box with a pain
  button; early on the ball is huge and hits the button constantly; over time the
  ball shrinks (or the box grows), so the pain is hit less often — but a random
  hit can still be just as sharp.

## How to grieve / support (the video's close)
- There's no "right" way or timeline; be gentle and patient with yourself.
- Feel it, don't suppress it; let the waves move through; lean on people.
- **To support someone grieving:** don't try to fix it or rush them; don't say
  "they're in a better place" or "at least…"; just show up, listen, and say "I'm
  here." Presence over advice.
- If grief is overwhelming, frozen, or unrelenting for a very long time
  (complicated/prolonged grief), professional support genuinely helps — a gentle,
  non-alarming nudge, not a diagnosis.

## Tone
- Warm, slow, deeply compassionate; validating; never prescriptive. The core
  message: however you're grieving, you're doing it right, and you're not alone.
