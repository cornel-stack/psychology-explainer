# SOURCES — "Signs Someone Is Secretly Jealous of You"

Channel: **Dear Brain**. A "Signs & Decoders" video: 10 subtle behavioural tells of
**covert envy**, why it happens, and how to respond without shrinking yourself.
Firmly non-paranoid: signs are **patterns, not proof** — one-offs mean nothing, and
sometimes the "jealousy" we sense is our own insecurity. All art bespoke; two
characters throughout (you = hoodie, the envious one = suit/tee).

## The 10 signs
| # | Sign | What it looks like | Note |
|---|------|--------------------|------|
| 1 | **Backhanded compliments** | Praise with a hidden barb ("nice, for a beginner"). | Covert put-down. |
| 2 | **Downplaying your wins** | "It's not *that* big a deal." Deflating your good news. | Minimisation. |
| 3 | **Fake enthusiasm** | A tight, forced smile when you share something great. | Nonverbal leakage (Ekman). |
| 4 | **One-upping** | Whatever you did, they did bigger/first/harder. | Competitiveness as a scoreboard. |
| 5 | **Copying you** | Suddenly imitating your style, choices, ideas. | "Imitation + resentment." |
| 6 | **Badmouthing behind your back** | Warm to your face, critical to others. | Reputation undermining. |
| 7 | **Secret delight in your failures** | A flicker of pleasure when you stumble (schadenfreude). | Smith & Kim (2007). |
| 8 | **The cold shoulder after you win** | Distant/quiet right after your success. | Warmth inversely tracks your wins. |
| 9 | **Constant criticism / nitpicking** | Nothing you do is ever quite good enough. | Chronic fault-finding. |
| 10 | **The tells** | Side-eye, crossed arms, avoiding eye contact, a flat "congrats". | Nonverbal + warm-when-you're-down pattern. |

## Why it happens (its own chapter)
Envy = **upward social comparison + insecurity** (Festinger; Smith & Kim). It's
about **their** inner scoreboard, not your worth. Often unconscious; the person may
not even admit it to themselves.

## How to respond (the payoff)
- **Don't dim your light** to make them comfortable.
- **A little compassion** — envy usually means someone is hurting/insecure.
- **Keep your boundaries** — distance from those who can't be happy for you; keep the
  real friends who celebrate you.

## Precision caveats (handled in VO)
- These are **patterns, not a diagnosis** — look for *repeated* behaviour, not a
  single bad day.
- Check yourself first: sometimes we project our **own** insecurity and misread
  neutral behaviour as envy.
- Most people are genuinely happy for you; this is about spotting the few who aren't.

## Disclaimer (also in metadata)
Education and curiosity, not a tool to accuse people. Read patterns, not one-offs,
and be honest about your own insecurities too. The goal isn't suspicion — it's
protecting your peace and keeping the friends who truly root for you.
