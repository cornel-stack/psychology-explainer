## Description
Think of three couples you know. They run on completely different engines — one bickers
and is blissfully happy, one never fights and is quietly falling apart. For decades
psychologists have asked not which style is "right," but which couples actually last,
and why. This is a field guide to them.

The engines behind every couple:

0:00 Why the happiest couples aren't the calmest
The three STABLE types — validating (calm, heard, compromising) · volatile (fiery but
affectionate) · conflict-avoidant (peaceful, independent). The warning signs — Gottman's
Four Horsemen (criticism, defensiveness, stonewalling, contempt) and their antidotes.
The pursue-withdraw trap (anxious–avoidant). The secure couple + earned security. The
best-friends couple, the power couple, the enmeshed/codependent couple, and the quiet
drifters (on-again-off-again, opposites, growing apart). Then: what actually lasts.

🪞 The research punchline (John Gottman): style barely matters. What predicts lasting
love is the ratio of warmth to friction (about 5:1), the ability to repair after a
rupture, and turning toward each other's small bids for connection. All three are
learnable, starting tonight.

⚠️ None of these is a verdict or a diagnosis — they're mirrors. Most of us are more than
one over a lifetime. Notice your pattern, protect the warmth, learn to repair.
