# SOURCES — "Every Level of Overthinking Explained"

Channel: **Dear Brain**. "Every Level Of" grammar: overthinking as a **ladder** that
climbs from healthy reflection up into harmful rumination — then how to climb back
down. The levels are a teaching device, not a clinical scale. All art bespoke.

## The 7 levels (healthy → harmful)
| # | Level | What it is | Source |
|---|-------|-----------|--------|
| 1 | **The Ponder** | Useful, *finite* reflection that reaches a decision. (The healthy base.) | — |
| 2 | **The Replay** | Re-running a past moment ("I should've said…"). | Rumination (Nolen-Hoeksema). |
| 3 | **The What-Ifs** | Spinning future worst-case scenarios. | Worry / GAD cognition. |
| 4 | **Analysis Paralysis** | So many options you freeze and can't choose. | Choice overload (Iyengar & Lepper; Schwartz, *Paradox of Choice*). |
| 5 | **The Spiral** | One worry splits and multiplies, pulling you down. | Catastrophic-worry chaining. |
| 6 | **Catastrophizing** | Each link escalates to total disaster ("I'll end up alone and broke"). | Cognitive distortions (Beck/Burns). |
| 7 | **Rumination** | A stuck, repetitive, inescapable loop; strongly tied to depression/anxiety. | Nolen-Hoeksema, response-styles theory. |

## The way back down (the payoff chapter)
- **Name the level** you're on — labeling creates distance.
- **Thinking ≠ doing:** rumination spins in place; **one small action** breaks the
  loop (behavioral activation).
- **Step back / zoom out:** "will this matter in a year?" shrinks the worry.
- **Set a worry limit / pull the brake:** schedule it, or just stop and redirect.
- **Good enough beats perfect:** paralysis dissolves when you lower the bar to *start*.

## On-screen numbers (StatCards)
- **~73%** of adults aged 25–35 identify as **overthinkers** (and ~52% of 45–55) —
  from Susan Nolen-Hoeksema's widely-cited surveys. Framed as "most of us."

## Precision caveats (handled in VO)
- The 7 "levels" are a **spectrum/ladder metaphor**, not a diagnosis; people move up
  and down it daily.
- Rumination at a clinical level (and analysis paralysis that halts your life) can
  need real help — stated.
- The 73% figure is one survey estimate, framed loosely ("most of us").

## Disclaimer (also in metadata)
Education and curiosity, not therapy. Everyone overthinks sometimes — it's normal.
But if rumination, worry, or indecision are seriously affecting your mood, sleep, or
life, a therapist can help far more than any video. You are not your thoughts.
