# Sources — Every Cognitive Distortion Explained

**Cognitive distortions** are habitual, biased ways of thinking — inaccurate
internal "self-talk" that fuels painful emotions. Core concept of **Cognitive
Behavioural Therapy (CBT)**: Aaron T. Beck identified distorted automatic thoughts
in depression; David D. Burns popularised the list in *Feeling Good* (1980).

Distinct from the previous "logical fallacies" video: fallacies are flaws in an
*argument with others*; distortions are flaws in *how we talk to ourselves*.

## The ten covered (Burns' classic list)
1. **All-or-Nothing (black-and-white) thinking** — seeing in absolutes; one slip =
   total failure.
2. **Overgeneralisation** — one event becomes a never-ending pattern ("always,"
   "never," "everyone").
3. **Mental Filter** — fixating on a single negative, filtering out everything good.
4. **Discounting the Positive** — insisting good things "don't count."
5. **Jumping to Conclusions (mind-reading / fortune-telling)** — assuming you know
   others' thoughts, or predicting the worst, with no evidence.
6. **Magnification / Catastrophising** — blowing things wildly out of proportion.
7. **Emotional Reasoning** — "I feel it, therefore it's true" (I feel like a
   failure, so I must be one).
8. **Should Statements** — rigid rules ("I should/must/ought"), breeding guilt and
   pressure.
9. **Labelling** — attaching a fixed, global label to yourself or others ("I'm a
   loser") instead of describing a behaviour.
10. **Personalisation & Blame** — taking responsibility for things outside your
    control; making it all about you.

## The reframe method (CBT)
- **The cognitive triangle:** thoughts ↔ feelings ↔ behaviours all influence each
  other; changing the thought shifts the whole loop.
- **Catch · Check · Change** — notice the automatic thought; examine the evidence
  for and against it; replace it with a more balanced, accurate one. (Cognitive
  restructuring; thought records.)

## Honest framing (stated in the video)
- Everyone uses these — they're normal mental shortcuts that misfire, **not signs
  of stupidity or weakness.** The goal is awareness, not self-criticism (which is
  itself often labelling + should-statements).
- CBT and cognitive restructuring are evidence-based and genuinely helpful, but
  this video is **psycho-education, not therapy or a diagnosis.** If distorted
  thinking is tied to persistent depression or anxiety, we gently encourage
  reaching out to a qualified professional — self-help plus real support.
- Reframing is NOT forced positivity or "just think happy." It's about being
  *accurate* and *fair* to yourself, not relentlessly upbeat.
