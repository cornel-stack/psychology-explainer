# SOURCES — "Every Cognitive Bias Ruining Your Decisions"

Channel: **Dear Brain**. Every claim traces here. Core program: the
**heuristics-and-biases** work of **Amos Tversky & Daniel Kahneman** (Kahneman won
the 2002 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics; *Thinking, Fast and Slow*, 2011).
Numbers are stated conservatively with the caveats below.

| # | Bias | Source | On-screen anchor (verified) |
|---|------|--------|------------------------------|
| 1 | **Anchoring** | Tversky & Kahneman (1974), *Science* 185 | Rigged wheel landed on **10** or **65**; median UN-countries estimate was **25%** vs **45%** — a random number moved answers 20 points. |
| 2 | **Sunk cost** | Arkes & Blumer (1985), *OBHDP* 35 | Ohio theater: buyers who paid **full price** attended **more** plays than those given a discount — same seats, more "investment." |
| 3 | **Confirmation bias** | Wason (1960), *QJEP* 12 | The **2-4-6 task**: people tested only triples that *confirmed* their guess; the rule was just "any ascending numbers." |
| 4 | **Availability heuristic** | Tversky & Kahneman (1973), *Cognitive Psychology* 5 | Most people think more words start with **K** than have K third — but there are ~**twice as many** with K in third place. |
| 5 | **Dunning–Kruger** | Kruger & Dunning (1999), *JPSP* 77 (Cornell) | Bottom-quartile scorers (~**12th** percentile) rated themselves ~**62nd** percentile. |
| 6 | **Loss aversion** | Kahneman & Tversky (1979), *Econometrica* (concept); λ≈**2.25** from Tversky & Kahneman (1992), *J. Risk & Uncertainty* | Losses feel about **twice** as painful as equal gains. (λ=2.25 is the 1992 estimate; the *concept* is 1979.) |
| 7 | **Halo effect** | Thorndike (1920) coined it; Nisbett & Wilson (1977), *JPSP* 35 | Same instructor filmed **warm** vs **cold**: viewers rated his *looks & accent* appealing or irritating to match — unaware. |
| 8 | **Framing effect** | Tversky & Kahneman (1981), *Science* 211 | "Asian disease": **72%** took the sure option framed as lives *saved*; ~**78%** gambled when the identical odds were framed as *deaths*. |
| 9 | **Hindsight bias** | Fischhoff (1975), *JEP:HPP* 1 | Told an outcome, people rated it as having been far more **predictable** than those not told — "I knew it all along." |
| 10 | **Bandwagon / conformity** | Asch (1951), Swarthmore | ~**75%** of people conformed to an obviously wrong group answer **at least once**; about **a third** of critical trials. Control error <1%. |
| 11 | **Survivorship bias** | Abraham **Wald**, WWII, Statistical Research Group (Columbia) | Bullet-hole maps of *returning* bombers → Wald said armor the spots with **no** holes; planes hit there never came back. |
| 12 | **Negativity bias** | Baumeister, Bratslavsky, Finkenauer & Vohs (2001), *Review of General Psychology* 5 | "**Bad is stronger than good**" across events, relationships, learning — one of psychology's most-cited papers. |
| 13 | **Optimism bias** | Tali Sharot (2011), *Current Biology* 21 / *The Optimism Bias* (UCL) | Sharot estimates ~**80%** of people show it — "it won't happen to me." |
| 14 | **Spotlight effect** | Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky (2000), *JPSP* 78 (Cornell) | Wearers of an embarrassing shirt guessed ~**half** of people noticed; only ~**a quarter** did. |
| 15 | **Barnum / Forer effect** | Forer (1949), *J. Abnormal & Social Psych.* 44 | **39** students rated an identical, astrology-book personality sketch ~**4.26 / 5** "accurate about me." |

## Precision caveats (phrased carefully on-screen)
- **Loss aversion:** "about twice" / "λ≈2.25" attributed to **1992**; concept to 1979.
- **Availability (K):** stated as "most people" + the solid "~2× more K-third" ratio.
- **Confirmation (2-4-6):** "people tested only confirming cases" (no hard % claimed).
- **Asch:** "about a third of the time" (the ~37% figure is the high end).
- **Optimism ~80%:** attributed to **Sharot's** characterization, not a single trial.
- **Spotlight:** "half vs a quarter" (≈46% vs 23% in the primary paper).
