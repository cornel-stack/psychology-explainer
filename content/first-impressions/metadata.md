## Description
You walk into a room and, before you've said a word, everyone has quietly made up
their mind about you. First impressions are fast, stubborn, and often wrong — yet they
shape job offers, friendships, verdicts, and love. So let's pull back the curtain on
them.

This is the science of the snap judgment:

0:00 Judged before you speak
Faster than thought (a tenth of a second) · thin-slicing (two seconds is enough) · the
first thing wins (the primacy effect) · the halo (one good trait glows) · the two
questions (warmth, then competence) · why they stick (confirmation + the self-
fulfilling prophecy) · making a good one (lead with warmth, the pratfall effect) · and
the second look.

👋 You can't stop your brain making snap judgments — but you can lead with warmth, let
your humanity show, and hold your own verdicts loosely. The best people in your life
are almost always the ones you took time to read past the opening line. (Not a rule
about any individual — snap judgments carry real bias; the useful move is awareness.)
