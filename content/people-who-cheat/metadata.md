## Description
Few things break a person like the discovery of betrayal — the message you weren't
meant to see, and the question that loops at 3am: *why?* The answer most people reach
for is simple: because they're a bad person, or the relationship was broken. But the
real psychology is far more complicated, and far more human, than that.

A careful, compassionate look at infidelity:

0:00 The question that loops at 3am
The myth of one reason · seeking a self (Esther Perel's reframe) · the web of motives
(unmet needs, validation, novelty, revenge, opportunity) · attachment & escape ·
emotional vs physical affairs · the slippery slope · the cost to the betrayed ·
understanding ≠ excusing · and whether it can survive.

💔 Understanding why people cheat is NOT the same as excusing it — the behaviour can be
understandable in human terms *and* still a betrayal, *and* entirely their
responsibility. If you were betrayed: it was never about what was wrong with you. The
decision to deceive belonged entirely to the one who made it. Affairs grow in neglect
and silence — so turn toward the people you love, name your needs, and speak hard truths
rather than acting them out.

⚠️ Not a diagnosis and no judgement of anyone's choices — if you're living through this
on either side, please seek real support. It's one of the hardest human things.
