# SOURCES — "Every Attachment Style Explained"

Channel: **Dear Brain**. Framing: **John Bowlby** proposed attachment theory —
the child needs a caregiver as a *safe base* (Bowlby, *Attachment and Loss*,
1969). **Mary Ainsworth** operationalized it with the **Strange Situation**
(Ainsworth et al., *Patterns of Attachment*, 1978), identifying **three** infant
patterns; **Main & Solomon** (1986/1990) added a **fourth**, *disorganized*.
**Hazan & Shaver** (1987) carried the patterns into **adult romantic love**;
**Bartholomew & Horowitz** (1991) gave the four-category adult model, later recast
as two dimensions — **anxiety × avoidance** (Brennan, Clark & Shaver, 1998, ECR).
Popular dynamics (protest behavior, deactivating strategies, the anxious–avoidant
trap) follow **Levine & Heller**, *Attached* (2010). Order in the video: origins →
the 4 styles → the dynamics → change.

## The four styles (adult terms)
| # | Style | Child analogue (Ainsworth/Main) | Core fear / move | Source |
|---|-------|--------------------------------|------------------|--------|
| 1 | **Secure** | secure | Comfortable with closeness *and* distance; names needs, repairs. | Ainsworth 1978; Hazan & Shaver 1987 |
| 2 | **Anxious / Preoccupied** | anxious-ambivalent (resistant) | "They'll leave." Hypervigilant, craves merger; **protest behavior**. | Bartholomew & Horowitz 1991; Levine & Heller 2010 |
| 3 | **Dismissive-Avoidant** | avoidant | "Needing people is dangerous." Self-reliant; **deactivates**, keeps distance. | Bartholomew & Horowitz 1991; Levine & Heller 2010 |
| 4 | **Fearful-Avoidant** | disorganized | Wants closeness *and* fears it; come-here / go-away; often from frightening caregiving. | Main & Solomon 1990; Bartholomew & Horowitz 1991 |

## Dynamics covered (sourced concepts, not invented styles)
- **Secure base / Strange Situation** — Bowlby 1969; Ainsworth 1978 (reunion
  behavior is the diagnostic moment).
- **Anxious–avoidant trap** — pursuer/distancer pairing; each confirms the other's
  fear (Levine & Heller 2010; also "pursue–withdraw" in couples research,
  Gottman; Christensen's demand/withdraw pattern).
- **Protest behavior** (anxious) & **deactivating strategies** (avoidant) — Levine
  & Heller 2010 (building on Cassidy & Kobak; Main).
- **How each loves / fights** — demand–withdraw loop; secure "repairs."
- **Earned security** — attachment is revisable; a coherent life narrative or a
  secure partner / therapy can move someone toward security (Main & Goldwyn, Adult
  Attachment Interview; Roisman et al. on *earned-secure*). Stability is moderate,
  not fixed (Fraley, longitudinal reviews).

## On-screen numbers (StatCards) — adult prevalence, Hazan & Shaver (1987)
- **~50% secure** (their samples ≈ 56%; framed as "about half").
- **~20% anxious** (≈ 19%; "roughly one in five").
- **~25% avoidant** (≈ 23–25%; "about a quarter").
> These proportions **vary by sample, culture, age, and measure** (self-report vs
> interview) — presented as rough, well-replicated ballparks, not fixed facts.

## Precision caveats (handled in VO)
- **Dismissive vs fearful avoidant** are distinct (low vs high anxiety); not lumped
  as one "avoidant."
- **Disorganized/fearful** origins stated as *often* linked to frightening/
  inconsistent caregiving — a risk factor, **not** a diagnosis or blame.
- Percentages framed as "about / roughly," never precise.
- "Earned secure" presented as realistic but gradual — no overnight-fix promise.

## Disclaimer (also in metadata)
Education and curiosity, not therapy or diagnosis. Attachment styles are patterns
on a spectrum, they shift over time, and most people are a mix. Nothing here
diagnoses you, your partner, or your parents.
