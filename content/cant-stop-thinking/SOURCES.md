# Sources — Why You Can't Stop Thinking About Someone

Why a single person can loop endlessly in your mind — the cognitive and emotional
mechanics of intrusive, repetitive thoughts about a specific someone (a crush, an ex,
someone who got away, an unresolved relationship) — and how to loosen the loop.
Framed as everyday psycho-education, not therapy or diagnosis.

## Why the loop forms
- **The Zeigarnik effect** (Bluma Zeigarnik, 1927): the mind keeps unfinished tasks
  and unresolved situations active in memory far more than completed ones. An
  ambiguous, unfinished, or un-closed relationship is an "open loop" the brain
  refuses to drop — it keeps pinging for resolution.
- **Intermittent reinforcement / variable-ratio reward** (Skinner): unpredictable
  rewards are the most powerfully habit-forming schedule of all. A person who is
  sometimes warm, sometimes distant (hot-and-cold, mixed signals, sporadic texts)
  trains the dopamine system exactly like a slot machine — the uncertainty is the
  hook, not the reward itself.
- **Dopamine = wanting, not having** (Berridge & Robinson's wanting-vs-liking):
  dopamine drives pursuit and anticipation, not satisfaction. Longing for someone
  you can't fully have keeps the "wanting" system firing — which feels like craving,
  not contentment.
- **Idealization & the pedestal:** absence and longing let us edit the person into a
  highlight reel. We miss our *image* of them — curated, best-moments-only — more
  than the full, flawed reality. (Linked to rosy-retrospection memory bias.)
- **Scarcity & reactance** (Brehm's reactance; scarcity principle): what we can't
  have becomes more desirable; a withdrawn or forbidden option gains value simply
  because it's out of reach ("the one that got away").
- **Rumination** (Nolen-Hoeksema): repetitive, passive dwelling on a distressing
  theme. The brain treats an unresolved social bond as a threat/problem to be solved
  and loops on it — but rumination rarely solves anything; it entrenches the thought.
- **Attachment & the nervous system:** for anxious-leaning attachment especially,
  separation or uncertainty can register as a survival alarm, driving
  preoccupation and protest behaviour. (See the attachment-styles episode.)
- **Memory & cue-triggered recall:** songs, places, objects and routines become
  retrieval cues that re-fire the thought automatically; the more you rehearse the
  memory, the stronger and more accessible the trace becomes.

## How the loop loosens (evidence-aligned, gentle)
- **Close the loop where you can:** seek realistic closure / acceptance; the goal is
  to let the brain mark the situation "resolved," even if resolution is just
  acceptance that it's over.
- **Reduce the cues (and the slot machine):** less checking, less contact, fewer
  reminders — intermittent checking keeps the variable-reward loop alive.
- **Reality-test the idealization:** recall the whole person, not the highlight reel.
- **Redirect, don't suppress:** thought suppression rebounds (Wegner's "white bear");
  gently redirecting attention and getting absorbed in other activity works better.
- **Self-compassion + time:** intrusive thoughts about someone fade with distance and
  new experiences; being harsh with yourself for still thinking of them only adds a
  second layer of distress. Time and self-kindness do the quiet work.

## Honest framing (stated in the video)
- Everyday, relatable framing — crushes, exes, unresolved connections — not clinical.
- Distinguish ordinary preoccupation from distress that's taking over daily life: if
  thoughts about someone become obsessive, compulsive, or involve someone who's
  unsafe (e.g. after a breakup involving abuse, or anything toward stalking), that's
  a sign to reach out to a professional. Not a diagnosis; support is available.
