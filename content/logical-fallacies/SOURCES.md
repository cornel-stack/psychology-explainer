# Sources — Every Logical Fallacy Explained

A **logical fallacy** is a flaw in *reasoning* — an argument that may sound
persuasive but doesn't actually support its conclusion. Distinct from a factual
error: a fallacy is about the *structure* of the argument, not whether a premise
happens to be true. Standard material from critical-thinking / informal-logic
curricula (e.g. Walton's *Informal Logic*; Damer, *Attacking Faulty Reasoning*;
university critical-thinking courses).

## The twelve covered (informal fallacies)
1. **Strawman** — misrepresent someone's argument into a weaker, distorted version,
   then knock *that* down. "So you're saying we should have no rules at all?"
2. **Ad hominem** — attack the person instead of their argument. "You'd say that,
   you failed maths." Latin: "to the person."
3. **Slippery slope** — claim one small step must inevitably cascade into an
   extreme outcome, with no evidence for the chain. "If we allow this, soon…"
4. **False dilemma (false dichotomy)** — present only two options when more exist.
   "You're either with us or against us."
5. **Appeal to authority** — treat a claim as true merely because an authority (or
   celebrity) asserts it, especially outside their expertise. *(Citing genuine
   relevant expertise is NOT a fallacy — the flaw is misusing authority.)*
6. **Bandwagon (appeal to popularity)** — "everyone believes it, so it's true."
   Popularity isn't evidence.
7. **Red herring** — introduce an irrelevant distraction to derail the argument.
8. **Circular reasoning (begging the question)** — the conclusion is smuggled into
   the premise. "It's the best because it's the best."
9. **Hasty generalisation** — draw a broad conclusion from too small/unrepresentative
   a sample. "My one bad experience proves they're all like that."
10. **Appeal to emotion** — substitute an emotional pull (fear, pity, pride) for a
    reason. *(Emotions aren't banned from argument — the flaw is using them
    *instead* of evidence.)*
11. **Tu quoque ("you too" / appeal to hypocrisy)** — deflect criticism by accusing
    the critic of the same fault, rather than addressing the point.
12. **False cause (post hoc / correlation≠causation)** — assume that because two
    things occur together or in sequence, one caused the other.

## Supporting concepts (diagrams)
- **Argument anatomy:** a claim stands on evidence + reasoning; attack a leg to
  test it (Toulmin-style).
- **Attack the argument, not the person** ("play the ball, not the player") — the
  antidote to ad hominem.
- **Correlation ≠ causation** — the core of false cause.
- **Spot-it checklist** — quick red-flags (attacking the person? only two options?
  tugging emotions? dodging the point?).

## Framing (stated in the video)
- Tone is empowering and non-smug: we ALL use these, often unintentionally, and
  the goal is clearer thinking (yours and others'), not winning internet fights.
- Two honest caveats included: citing *relevant* expertise is legitimate (not an
  authority fallacy), and emotions have a real place in persuasion — the fallacy
  is using them *in place of* a reason. "Fallacy fallacy" nod: a bad argument for
  a claim doesn't make the claim false.
