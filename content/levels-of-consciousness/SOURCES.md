# Sources — Every Level of Consciousness Explained

"Consciousness" is used here in the everyday psychological sense — *how aware you
are, and of what* — not the hard philosophical problem of why experience exists
at all. We present a **ladder of awareness states**, from none to peak, as an
accessible map. It is explicitly **not a single clinical scale**; it braids
together several real ideas:

## The ideas braided together
- **Arousal / levels of consciousness (clinical):** the continuum from coma →
  stupor → drowsiness → alertness, assessed with tools like the Glasgow Coma
  Scale. Grounds the low end (unconscious) and the "one sliding scale" framing.
- **Sleep & dreaming:** REM dreaming as consciousness "turned inward" — awareness
  without the outside world. (Jouvet; Hobson.)
- **Default Mode Network / mind-wandering:** the brain's autopilot when not
  focused on the outside — Killingsworth & Gilbert, "a wandering mind is an
  unhappy mind" (2010); Raichle's DMN.
- **Metacognition:** awareness of one's own thinking — Flavell (1979).
- **Self-awareness:** recognising oneself as an object of attention — Duval &
  Wicklund's objective self-awareness; the mirror test (Gallup).
- **Flow:** Mihály Csíkszentmihályi — total absorption where self-consciousness
  falls away, at the balance point of challenge and skill (the flow channel).
- **Peak / transcendent experience:** Maslow's "peak experiences"; William James,
  *The Varieties of Religious Experience* — handled as a natural, reported human
  state, with NO supernatural claims.
- **Freud's topography:** conscious / preconscious(subconscious) / unconscious —
  the "mind's floors," most of it below the surface (the iceberg idea).

## The eight rungs (as presented, low → high)
1. **Unconscious** — coma / dreamless sleep / anaesthesia: the lights are out.
2. **Dreaming** — awareness turned inward, vivid but cut off from the world.
3. **Autopilot** — awake but mind-wandering; the body acts while attention drifts.
4. **Present (situational)** — grounded in the here-and-now, taking the world in.
5. **Self-aware** — aware of yourself as seen, as a self among others.
6. **Metacognition** — aware of your own thinking; watching your thoughts.
7. **Flow** — so absorbed the self dissolves into the action.
8. **Peak / clear awareness** — rare, expansive, lucid; the view from the summit.

## Honest framing (stated in the video)
- Repeated: "a map, not a measuring tape — not a clinical scale." The numbered
  rungs are a teaching device; real consciousness is a **dial, not a switch**,
  and you slide up and down it all day.
- Flow and peak states are framed as ordinary, reachable human experiences, not
  mystical achievements; no supernatural or medical claims.
- The low end (coma, anaesthesia) is handled plainly and without fear.
- Closes on the quiet "watcher" — the awareness that is present at every rung.
