## Description
A song on the radio, the smell of a certain summer, a slant of late-afternoon light —
and suddenly you're somewhere twenty years gone, chest aching, warm and sad at once.
That ache has a name: nostalgia. And it was once thought to be a disease.

This is the surprising, comforting science of looking back:

0:00 The ache with a name
Once called a disease (Hofer, 1688) · the bittersweet blend · it's actually good for
you (Southampton research) · the smell shortcut (the Proust effect) · the soundtrack
of you (music & the reminiscence bump) · why ages 10–30 stick · a bridge to others
(nostalgia fights loneliness) · the thread of you (self-continuity) · the built-in
comfort loop · and how to use it well.

💛 That wistful pull isn't weakness or sickness — it lifts mood, deepens meaning, and
reminds you that you belong. Visit the past often; just don't move in. (If looking
back brings only pain, or you can only live in the past, a kind professional can
help.)
