# Sources — The Psychology of Regret

The 3am "if only." Why regret aches so much, what the research says we regret most,
and why a feeling we try to avoid is actually one of our most useful. Warm, honest,
and ultimately hopeful. Psycho-education, not diagnosis.

## What regret is
- **Regret** is the painful emotion we feel when we imagine that a *better* outcome
  would have followed a *different* choice. Its engine is **counterfactual thinking** —
  "if only I had…" — comparing reality against an imagined alternative.
- **Upward counterfactuals** (imagining something better) feel bad but motivate and
  teach; **downward** ones (imagining worse) comfort us. Regret is the upward kind.

## It's one of our most common emotions
- Across studies, regret is among the **most frequently named negative emotions** in
  everyday life (Shimanoff; Morrison & Roese). Nearly everyone feels it, often.

## Actions vs inactions — the great shift over time
- **Short term, we regret our actions** (the embarrassing thing we did). **Long term,
  we regret our inactions far more** — the chances we didn't take, the things we
  didn't say (Gilovich & Medvec, 1995). "I wish I had…" outlasts "I wish I hadn't…".
- The regrets that linger longest are tied to the **ideal self** — who we aspired to
  become — more than the "ought self" of duties (Davidai & Gilovich, 2018). We make
  peace with failing our obligations; we keep aching over unrealised dreams.

## Why it stings most when you came close
- **Counterfactual closeness / the near-miss:** regret is sharpest when the better
  outcome was *almost* reached. Famously, **bronze medalists often look happier than
  silver** — silver compares up to gold ("if only…"), bronze compares down to no
  medal ("at least…") (Medvec, Madey & Gilovich, 1995).

## Regret is a teacher (its function)
- Regret is **adaptive**: it flags mistakes, sharpens future decisions, and motivates
  repair and change. People who **can't feel regret** — e.g. after damage to the
  orbitofrontal cortex (Camille et al., 2004) — make **worse** choices. The pain is
  the point: it's feedback.
- **Daniel Pink (*The Power of Regret*):** four core regrets — **foundation** (I wish
  I'd been responsible), **boldness** (I wish I'd taken the chance), **moral** (I wish
  I'd done the right thing), **connection** (I wish I'd reached out). They reveal what
  we value, and point to how to live.

## When regret turns toxic
- Regret curdles when it becomes **rumination** — endless self-blame with no lesson or
  action — which is linked to anxiety and depression. Healthy regret *informs*; toxic
  regret *punishes*.

## Using regret well (stated in the video)
- **Self-compassion** first (treat yourself as a friend; everyone has regrets).
- **Extract the lesson** — what does this regret tell me I value?
- **Act on it** — repair where you can, and let future choices be guided by it
  ("At Least" to soften the past; "If Only" to guide the future).
- Reframe: a life with *no* regret isn't a life well-lived — regret means you cared,
  grew, and have values. Honest caveat: if regret becomes a relentless, hopeless
  spiral, that can accompany depression and deserves professional support.
