## Description
You were having a perfectly fine day — then you picked up your phone. Someone's new
house, someone's dream holiday, someone your age already running a company. Ten minutes
later your fine life feels small and not quite enough. That's comparison, and it just
quietly robbed you.

This is the psychology of comparing yourself:

0:00 The scroll that shrinks you
It's automatic (social comparison theory) · up and down (the two directions) · the
rigged game (reel vs real life) · it steals your joy (the thief at work) · the endless
ladder (a race with no finish) · use it, don't be used (envy as information) · and four
ways to take back control.

🪞 You're comparing your messy, complete reality to a stranger's carefully chosen
highlight — it was never a fair fight, and never a race you had to run. Catch it,
curate your feed, compare to your *past self*, and run your own lane. You're not behind.
You're just on your own road.

⚠️ Comparing yourself is universal and normal — not a flaw. Not a diagnosis; but if
comparison is driving persistent misery or a collapse in self-worth, that's worth real
support.
