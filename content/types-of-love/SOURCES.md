# Sources — Every Type of Love Explained

A tour of the different kinds of love, framed around the **ancient Greek words for
love** (popularised in modern psychology and philosophy — e.g. C.S. Lewis, *The
Four Loves*; the "colours of love" research tradition, John Alan Lee's love
styles) and **Robert Sternberg's Triangular Theory of Love**.

## The eight loves (Greek)
1. **Eros** — passionate, romantic, sexual love; intense, intoxicating desire.
2. **Philia** — deep friendship; affectionate love between equals, loyalty, shared
   values.
3. **Storge** — familial love; the natural, enduring bond between family members.
4. **Agape** — selfless, unconditional, universal love; compassion for all; giving
   without expecting return.
5. **Ludus** — playful, flirtatious love; the fun, teasing early sparks; games and
   lightheartedness.
6. **Pragma** — mature, enduring, practical love; the deep understanding that
   grows over long years; "standing in love."
7. **Philautia** — self-love; in its healthy form, self-respect and self-compassion
   (not vanity); the foundation that lets you love others well.
8. **Mania** — obsessive, possessive love; the jealous, dependent, consuming kind;
   the shadow side (handled with care, with a nod toward unhealthy attachment).

## Sternberg's Triangular Theory
Three components — **Intimacy** (closeness), **Passion** (desire), **Commitment**
(the decision to stay) — combine into seven kinds of love:
- Intimacy only → **Liking** (friendship)
- Passion only → **Infatuation**
- Commitment only → **Empty love**
- Intimacy + Passion → **Romantic love**
- Intimacy + Commitment → **Companionate love**
- Passion + Commitment → **Fatuous love**
- All three → **Consummate love** (complete, lasting)

## Love over time
- Early **passionate love** (eros, high dopamine/norepinephrine, the "honeymoon")
  naturally cools over months to a couple of years; healthy relationships
  transition it into **companionate love** (deep attachment, oxytocin, trust).
  Spark cools; roots deepen. (Helen Fisher; Hatfield & Rapson.)

## Self-love first
- **Philautia as foundation:** healthy self-love and self-compassion (Neff) support
  the capacity to love others; "you can't pour from an empty cup." Distinguished
  clearly from narcissism/vanity.

## Honest framing (stated in the video)
- These categories overlap and blend — real relationships mix several at once, and
  the healthiest long-term bonds weave many together (eros + philia + pragma).
- Mania / obsessive love is handled gently as the unhealthy end, with a nod that if
  love feels consuming, jealous or controlling, that's worth tending (and a note
  that this is understanding, not therapy or a diagnosis).
- Warm, inclusive, dignity-first; love framed broadly (romantic, familial,
  platonic, self), not only couples.
