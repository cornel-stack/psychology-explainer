# Sources — Why Your Brain Procrastinates

The core, evidence-backed reframe: **procrastination is not a time-management or
laziness problem — it is an emotion-regulation problem.** We put off tasks to
escape the *negative feelings* they stir up (boredom, anxiety, self-doubt,
frustration), trading a worse future for a better-feeling now.

## Key ideas
- **Emotion regulation, not laziness:** Tim Pychyl & Fuschia Sirois — procrastination
  as "giving in to feel good," short-term mood repair at long-term cost.
  "Procrastination is an emotion-regulation problem, not a time-management
  problem."
- **Present bias / temporal discounting:** we steeply discount future rewards and
  costs; the vivid NOW outweighs an abstract LATER. (Hyperbolic discounting —
  Ainslie, Laibson.) The "present self vs future self" framing.
- **Limbic system vs prefrontal cortex:** the impulsive, emotional limbic brain
  (wants relief now) versus the planning prefrontal cortex (holds the long-term
  goal). Under stress or low effort, the limbic side tends to win. (Steel's
  Temporal Motivation Theory; McClure et al. on immediate vs delayed reward.)
- **Negative reinforcement / the avoidance loop:** avoiding the task brings
  instant relief, which *reinforces* the avoidance — so the habit strengthens
  each time, while the task (and dread) grows.
- **Task aversiveness:** the more boring, ambiguous, difficult, or
  ego-threatening a task feels, the more we avoid it (Steel's meta-analysis,
  2007).
- **Deadlines & panic:** looming consequences finally make the cost of *not*
  acting outweigh the discomfort of acting — hence the last-minute sprint. (The
  popular "panic monster" idea — we use an ORIGINAL "deadline beast" character,
  not derived from any existing meme art.)
- **The guilt spiral:** procrastinating → shame and self-criticism → worse mood →
  *more* need to avoid. Self-criticism fuels the cycle.
- **Self-compassion breaks it:** Sirois — self-forgiveness and self-compassion
  reduce future procrastination (students who forgave themselves for
  procrastinating studied more next time). Kristin Neff's self-compassion work.
- **The way out (behavioural):** shrink the task; the "two-minute rule" / just
  start (lower the activation energy); implementation intentions ("if-then"
  plans, Gollwitzer); make the first step tiny so the limbic brain doesn't panic.

## Honest framing (stated in the video)
- Chronic procrastination can be tied to anxiety, ADHD, or depression; the video
  notes this is about everyday procrastination and gently points anyone
  struggling badly toward real support — not a diagnosis.
- The central, dignity-first message: **you are not lazy or broken.** Your brain
  is doing something very human — protecting you from a bad feeling — just in a
  way that backfires. Naming the mechanism is the first step to working with it.
