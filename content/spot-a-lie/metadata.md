## Description
They're telling you a story and your gut says: that's not true. They glanced away,
they touched their nose — you know the tells. But here's the uncomfortable truth:
almost everything you think you know about spotting a liar is wrong.

This is the honest, myth-busting science of deception:

0:00 The gut feeling
We believe by default (the truth-default) · we're terrible at it (~54%, basically
a coin flip) · the myths that must die (there's no reliable body-language tell) ·
the Othello error (nervous ≠ lying) · microexpressions (real, but overhyped) · the
one real tell (lying is cognitively harder) · listen, don't watch · make it harder
(reverse order, unexpected questions) · and how to handle suspicion wisely.

🔎 You can't reliably read lies off a face — and acting like you can corrodes trust
and wrongs innocent people. Weigh the evidence, not the eyes. (Chronic suspicion of
a partner is its own pain and worth looking at gently, perhaps with support.)
