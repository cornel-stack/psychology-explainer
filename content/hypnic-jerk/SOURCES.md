# SOURCES — The Hypnic Jerk

Facts are stated conservatively and matched to the narration beats.

| Claim in script | Basis | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A "hypnic jerk" (a.k.a. hypnagogic jerk / sleep start) is an involuntary muscle twitch during the transition from wakefulness to sleep. | Well-established clinical description; a benign form of sleep-onset myoclonus. | AASM, *International Classification of Sleep Disorders* (ICSD-3), "sleep starts (hypnic jerks)". Cleveland Clinic, "Hypnic Jerks". |
| It occurs as you move from being awake into the first/light stages of sleep (N1). | Sleep-onset phenomenon at the wake→N1 transition. | Sleep Foundation, "Hypnic Jerks: Why You Twitch Before Falling Asleep" (reviewed). NIH/NHLBI sleep-stages overview. |
| On the way to sleep, muscle tone decreases and cortical/brain activity slows. | Normal physiology of sleep onset (descending arousal, reduced muscle tone). | NINDS "Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep". |
| ~70% of people have experienced a hypnic jerk. | Commonly cited prevalence is ~60–70% of the general population; we say "around seventy percent." | Sleep Foundation (cites ~70%); review literature on sleep starts. Treated as an estimate, not a precise figure. |
| One theory: the brain misinterprets muscle relaxation as falling and triggers a jolt (a reflexive "catch"). | A frequently cited hypothesis; mechanism is not definitively proven. Narration frames it as "one popular idea"/"theory", not fact. | Sleep Foundation; popular-science summaries of the "evolutionary/falling" hypothesis. |
| Hypnic jerks are harmless and need no treatment in the typical case. | Consensus for isolated, occasional hypnic jerks (not a disorder). | Cleveland Clinic; AASM ICSD-3 (benign). |

Pronunciation note: "hypnic" = HIP-nik. Related seeded lexicon terms (hypnagogic, myoclonus) are available in `scripts/lexicon.json` for future chapters.

Caveat: prevalence and the "falling misread" mechanism are reported as estimates/theories in the narration to stay within what the literature supports.
