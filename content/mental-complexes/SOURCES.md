# Sources — Every Mental Complex Explained

A "complex" in depth psychology = an organised cluster of emotionally charged
memories, beliefs and perceptions around a common theme, operating largely
outside awareness and pulling behaviour toward itself. The term is Jungian;
several of the named complexes below are popular/clinical shorthand rather than
formal DSM diagnoses. This script treats them as *patterns*, explicitly NOT as
diagnoses, and keeps the tone compassionate.

## Core concept
- C.G. Jung — *Psychological Types* (1921) and the "feeling-toned complex"
  (word-association experiments, Burghölzli). A complex is a "splinter psyche"
  with its own charge.
- Alfred Adler — *The Neurotic Constitution* / *Understanding Human Nature*:
  the **inferiority complex** and its compensation, the **superiority
  complex**. "To be a human being means to possess a feeling of inferiority
  that constantly presses towards its own conquer." Striving-for-significance.

## The fifteen
1. **Inferiority** — Adler: persistent, exaggerated sense of not-enough.
2. **Superiority** — Adler: a mask bolted over inferiority ("same wound,
   opposite mask").
3. **God complex** — colloquial: unshakeable belief in one's own infallibility
   / omnipotence (common critique in medicine, leadership).
4. **Savior / Messiah complex** — compulsion to rescue; self-worth tied to
   fixing others; overlaps "white-knight" dynamics.
5. **Hero complex** — need to be seen as the heroic rescuer, sometimes
   manufacturing crises to solve.
6. **Martyr complex** — deriving identity/worth from self-sacrifice and
   suffering; "after all I've done for you."
7. **Guilt complex** — chronic, often disproportionate guilt; feeling
   responsible for things outside one's control.
8. **Persecution complex** — pervasive belief that others are out to harm or
   undermine you (sub-clinical; distinct from clinical paranoia/delusion).
9. **Victim complex / victim mentality** — habitual framing of self as the
   wronged party; external locus of control.
10. **Napoleon complex** ("short-man syndrome") — popular idea of
    overcompensating for a perceived physical/social smallness; the empirical
    evidence is mixed — flagged in VO as folklore.
11. **Cinderella complex** — Colette Dowling (1981): an unconscious wish to be
    taken care of / fear of independence.
12. **Peter Pan complex** — Dan Kiley (1983): adult avoidance of the
    responsibilities of growing up (popular, not clinical).
13. **Oedipus / Electra complex** — Freud / Jung: childhood attachment pattern
    toward the opposite-sex parent; handled briefly and non-salaciously.
14. **Cassandra complex** — seeing/ warning of a truth and being disbelieved;
    named for the Trojan prophetess.
15. **Jonah complex** — Abraham Maslow: the *fear of one's own greatness*;
    running from your potential (named for Jonah fleeing his calling).

## Framing devices (original art)
- Rooms: **masquerade-hall** (complexes as masks/personas), **pedestal-gallery**
  (self-image on plinths), **analysis-room** (the couch, where roots are seen).
- Diagrams: **complex-core** (a charged knot of belief/feeling/memory),
  **hidden-roots** (visible behaviour, buried cause), **overcompensate-arc**
  (inferiority↔superiority), **two-sides-coin** (pride & shame, one coin).

## Responsible-use notes
- Repeated on-screen and in VO: "patterns, not proof — not a diagnosis."
- Napoleon/Cinderella/Peter Pan flagged as popular labels; evidence caveated.
- Oedipus/Electra kept clinical and brief; no prurience.
- Close on agency: complexes are learned and *workable*; naming is the first
  loosening of the knot.
