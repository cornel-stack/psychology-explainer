# Sources — Why Your Brain Feels Foggy

What brain fog actually is (a cluster of symptoms, not a disease), the main physical
and psychological causes of cloudy, slow, forgetful thinking, and how to clear it.
Practical, evidence-informed, honest. Not a diagnosis — persistent fog deserves a
doctor's visit.

## What brain fog is
- "Brain fog" isn't a medical diagnosis — it's a common, informal description of a
  **cluster of cognitive symptoms**: difficulty concentrating, mental **slowness**,
  **forgetfulness**, trouble finding words, and a hazy, "cotton wool" feeling. It's
  about **clarity**, not just energy — you can feel foggy without feeling sleepy.
- Crucially, it's almost always a **symptom of something else** — a signal that some
  system (sleep, stress, inflammation, blood sugar, hormones, mood) is off.

## Sleep debt — the biggest culprit
- Poor or insufficient sleep hammers the very systems fog depends on: **attention,
  working memory, and processing speed**. Deep sleep clears metabolic waste (the
  **glymphatic system**) and consolidates memory; shortchange it and thinking turns
  slow and hazy. Fragmented sleep (apnoea, late screens) counts even if hours look fine.

## Chronic stress and cortisol
- Sustained stress keeps **cortisol** elevated, which impairs the **prefrontal cortex**
  (focus, planning) and the **hippocampus** (memory). A brain stuck in low-grade threat
  spends its resources scanning for danger, not thinking clearly — producing that
  scattered, can't-hold-a-thought fog.

## Inflammation & being unwell (incl. post-viral)
- **Inflammation** — from illness, infection, or chronic conditions — produces
  **"sickness behaviour,"** including mental sluggishness; inflammatory signalling
  (cytokines) directly dampens cognition. **Post-viral fog** (notably after COVID-19)
  is a well-documented, real example. Autoimmune conditions and chronic illness can do
  the same.

## Blood sugar, hydration, and nutrition
- The brain is a glucose-hungry organ; **blood-sugar swings** (big sugary spikes then
  crashes) leave thinking jittery then foggy. Even **mild dehydration** measurably
  impairs attention and short-term memory. Deficiencies (e.g. **B12, iron, vitamin D**,
  omega-3s) are classic, treatable causes of persistent fog.

## Hormones
- Hormonal shifts strongly affect cognition: **thyroid** problems (hypothyroidism),
  **perimenopause / menopause** (oestrogen changes), pregnancy, and the menstrual cycle
  are all common, legitimate sources of fog — often overlooked and very treatable.

## Information overload & multitasking
- The modern mind faces relentless **input and task-switching**. **Multitasking is a
  myth** — the brain rapidly switches, leaving **attention residue** and a drained,
  scattered feeling. Constant notifications and open loops fragment focus into fog.

## Anxiety, depression, and mood
- **Anxiety** floods working memory with worry, leaving little bandwidth for clear
  thought. **Depression** commonly includes genuine cognitive symptoms — slowed
  thinking, poor concentration, memory lapses — not laziness. Mood and clarity are
  tightly linked.

## The gut and movement
- The **gut–brain axis** means poor gut health and a highly processed diet can
  contribute to fog. And **physical inactivity** worsens it: exercise boosts blood flow
  and **BDNF** (a brain-growth factor), sharpening thinking — one of the most reliable
  fog-lifters.

## How to clear it (stated in the video)
- **Protect sleep** first — it's the highest-leverage fix.
- **Move daily** — even a brisk walk measurably clears fog.
- **Hydrate and steady your blood sugar** (protein/fibre over sugar spikes).
- **Lower the input** — single-task, cut multitasking, take real breaks from screens.
- **Manage stress** — the fog often lifts when the pressure does.
- **Get daylight** and basic nutrition sorted.

## Honest caveat
- Occasional fog is normal. But **persistent, worsening, or sudden** brain fog deserves
  a **doctor's visit** — it can flag thyroid issues, deficiencies, sleep disorders,
  depression, long-COVID, or other treatable conditions. **Not a diagnosis.** Foggy
  thinking is your brain asking for support, not a sign you're broken or "losing it."
