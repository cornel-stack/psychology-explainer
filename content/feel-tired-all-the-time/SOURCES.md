# Sources — Why You Feel Tired All the Time

Why you can sleep eight hours and still feel exhausted — the psychological and
lifestyle drains behind modern tiredness, and the kind of rest that actually refills
you. Practical, warm, evidence-informed. Not medical advice — persistent fatigue
deserves a doctor's visit to rule out physical causes.

## Tiredness isn't only about sleep
- You can get "enough" sleep and still feel drained, because fatigue has **physical,
  mental, and emotional** forms. Much of modern, chronic tiredness is **not** physical
  exhaustion from exertion — it's **mental and emotional depletion** that sleep alone
  doesn't fix.

## Cognitive load and decision fatigue
- The brain is metabolically expensive, and **sustained mental effort feels tiring**.
  A constant stream of **decisions, notifications, and task-switching** drains our
  limited capacity ("**decision fatigue**"). Every switch leaves **attention residue**
  (Sophie Leroy) — part of your mind stuck on the last task — so you feel foggy and
  spent without having "done" much physically.

## Chronic stress — "tired but wired"
- Ongoing, low-grade stress keeps the body in mild **fight-or-flight**, with elevated
  **cortisol and adrenaline**. Over time this **allostatic load** is exhausting — and
  paradoxically it can leave you **"tired but wired"**: depleted yet unable to properly
  rest or switch off.

## Emotional labour and suppression
- **Emotional labour** — managing your feelings and expressions for others (masking,
  people-pleasing, "service with a smile") — is genuinely **effortful**. Chronically
  **suppressing emotion** carries a measurable cognitive and physiological cost; holding
  it together all day is tiring in a way that doesn't show up as "doing" anything.

## Understimulation and languishing
- Fatigue isn't only from overload. **Monotony, boredom, and lack of meaning** drain us
  too. Psychologist Corey Keyes's concept of **languishing** — popularised by Adam Grant
  — describes a flat, joyless "meh" of **stagnation and absence of purpose** that feels
  a lot like tiredness, even when nothing is objectively wrong.

## Screens, dopamine, and "junk rest"
- Much of what we call resting — **passive scrolling**, binge-watching — fragments
  attention and overstimulates the reward system without actually **restoring** us. It's
  **"junk rest"**: it feels like a break but leaves you more depleted, the mental
  equivalent of junk food.

## We rest wrong — the seven types of rest
- Dr **Saundra Dalton-Smith** argues most of us are **rest-deprived in specific ways**
  and try to fix every kind of tiredness with sleep. She describes **seven types of
  rest**: **physical, mental, sensory, creative, emotional, social, and spiritual.**
  If you're drained in one domain, sleep in another won't help — you have to **match the
  rest to the deficit** (e.g. sensory rest from screens, social rest from draining
  people, emotional rest from masking).

## What actually restores energy
- Counter-intuitively, **movement creates energy** (light exercise reliably reduces
  fatigue). **Morning daylight** anchors the circadian clock and improves alertness and
  sleep. **Genuine connection**, time in nature, single-tasking, and **meaning/purpose**
  are energising. Basic inputs — **hydration, nutrition, daylight** — matter.

## Honest caveat
- Persistent, unexplained fatigue can also signal **medical issues** (thyroid, anaemia,
  sleep apnoea, long-term conditions) or **depression / burnout**, where low energy,
  low mood, and loss of interest run together. **Not a diagnosis.** If your tiredness is
  severe, lasting, or comes with low mood, please **see a doctor** — it's a real symptom
  worth taking seriously, not a character flaw.
