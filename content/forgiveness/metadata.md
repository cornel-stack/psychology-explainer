## Description
There's a conversation you keep having in your head — with the person who betrayed you,
who lied, who left. You rehearse it at 3am and win the argument every time. And every
time, you lose, because they're not even there. You're the only one still in that room.
Forgiveness is how you finally walk out.

This is the real psychology of letting go:

0:00 Still in that room
What it is NOT (not forgetting, excusing, or reconciling) · the grudge hurts you (the
burning coal) · what it does to your body (the hidden bill) · why it's so hard (the
replay loop) · forgive AND protect (boundaries stay up) · how to forgive (the REACH
model) · and the hardest one: forgiving yourself.

💚 Forgiveness was never about them — it's you putting down the heavy thing and getting
your hands, and your heart, back. You can release the bitterness and keep the boundary.
You don't need their apology. And you can do it slowly, at your own pace.

⚠️ Forgiveness is not condoning harm, and never requires contacting or reconciling with
an abuser — safety and boundaries come first. It's a process, not an event. Not a
diagnosis; for deep trauma, a good therapist can help you walk this well.

Welcome to Dear Brain — calm, illustrated psychology, one idea at a time.
