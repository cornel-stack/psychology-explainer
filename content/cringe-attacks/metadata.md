## Description
You're in the shower, minding your own business, when it strikes — a memory from
fourteen years ago, and your whole body physically recoils. A cringe attack. And the
person it was about? They forgot it the same day.

This is the surprisingly comforting science of cringe:

0:00 The 2am replay
A self + an audience (the self-conscious emotions) · why they stick (emotion burns
memory in) · an ancient alarm (belonging meant survival) · why they ambush you (the
wandering mind) · your only audience (the spotlight effect) · memory lies (it
inflates the badness) · cringing means you care · and how to defuse a cringe attack.

💛 Cringe attacks aren't proof you're awkward — they're proof you have empathy,
self-awareness and a conscience. Almost nobody else remembers; you're your own
harshest audience. (If they become relentless or tie into social anxiety that
shrinks your life, a professional can genuinely help.)

Welcome to Dear Brain — calm, illustrated psychology, one idea at a time.
