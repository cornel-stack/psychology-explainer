# Sources — Every Type of Intelligence Explained

Based on **Howard Gardner's theory of Multiple Intelligences** (*Frames of Mind*,
1983; updated in *Intelligence Reframed*, 1999). Gardner argued that "intelligence"
isn't one general number (IQ / Spearman's g) but a set of relatively independent
capacities, each with its own developmental path and neural basis.

## The intelligences (as presented — 8 core + 1)
1. **Linguistic** (word smart) — facility with language, words, writing, speaking.
2. **Logical-Mathematical** (logic smart) — reasoning, patterns, numbers, logic.
3. **Spatial** (picture smart) — visualising, mental rotation, maps, design.
4. **Musical** (music smart) — rhythm, pitch, melody, musical patterns.
5. **Bodily-Kinesthetic** (body smart) — body control, coordination, physical skill.
6. **Interpersonal** (people smart) — reading, understanding and working with others.
7. **Intrapersonal** (self smart) — self-knowledge, understanding one's own inner life.
8. **Naturalistic** (nature smart) — recognising and classifying the natural world
   (added by Gardner in 1995).
9. **Existential** (meaning smart) — the "candidate" ninth: grappling with big
   questions of meaning, death, existence. Gardner has treated it cautiously.

## Honest framing (stated in the video)
- Multiple Intelligences is **influential and widely loved by educators, but
  scientifically debated.** Many psychologists argue these are better described as
  *talents*, *aptitudes* or *skills* rather than separate "intelligences," and that
  the theory has limited direct empirical support; the mainstream science still
  finds a strong general-intelligence factor (g). We present MI as a **useful,
  humane lens** for seeing the breadth of human ability — not as settled fact.
- The **"learning styles" myth is explicitly NOT endorsed.** Gardner himself has
  said MI is not the same as learning styles, and the idea that you must be taught
  in "your style" is not supported by evidence. We avoid that claim.
- The uplifting, accurate core that survives the debate: **intelligence is plural
  and broad.** A single test score captures only a slice of what a mind can do, and
  nearly everyone is strong somewhere. That's the message.

## Supporting concepts (diagrams)
- **Intelligence wheel** — one mind, many spokes.
- **Not one number** — a single IQ score can't capture the whole spread.
- **Spiky profile** — everyone has a different shape of strengths and weaknesses.
- **Strength-finder** — spot your top few, then deliberately grow them.

## Tone
- Celebratory and empowering; the aim is to help people (especially those who felt
  "not smart" in a school built around only two of these) recognise where they
  genuinely shine. Dignity-first, no one's profile framed as better than another's.
