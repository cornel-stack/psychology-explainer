## Description
For a hundred years we crammed intelligence into one narrow box — a single IQ
number from a single kind of test. But that score only ever captured a thin slice
of what a mind can do. Whole oceans of brilliance fell straight through the net.

Based on Howard Gardner's theory of Multiple Intelligences, this episode explores
nine different kinds of smart — so you can find the one or two where you quietly
shine (maybe ones a classroom never had a test for):

0:00 Intelligence isn't one number
1 Word smart (linguistic)
2 Logic smart (logical-mathematical)
3 Picture smart (spatial)
4 Music smart (musical)
5 Body smart (bodily-kinesthetic)
6 People smart (interpersonal)
7 Self smart (intrapersonal)
8 Nature smart (naturalistic)
9 Meaning smart (existential)
…plus your "spiky profile" and how to find and grow your strengths.

🧠 An honest note: Multiple Intelligences is loved by educators but debated by
scientists — hold it as a useful, humane lens, not settled fact. (And no, this
isn't the "learning styles" myth — Gardner rejects that too.) The accurate core:
intelligence is plural, and nearly everyone is a genius at something.

Welcome to Dear Brain — calm, illustrated psychology, one idea at a time.
