# SOURCES — "The Psychology of People Who Don't Use Social Media"

Channel: **Dear Brain**. Creative framing: shot like a **nature documentary** — the
narrator observes the person who doesn't use social media as a rare "species" in a
habitat of hunched, glowing "glow-dwellers." The tone is warm and curious, **not
smug**: it's a portrait of real psychological tendencies, with honest trade-offs
and a myth-bust, not a claim that logging off makes anyone better than anyone.

## The 9 traits (each grounded in research)
| # | Trait | Psychology behind it | Source |
|---|-------|----------------------|--------|
| 1 | **Thinks in its own voice** | Opinions formed slowly from many sources, not an algorithmic feed that rewards outrage/conformity. | Attention-economy critiques (Tim Wu, *The Attention Merchants*; Tristan Harris / Center for Humane Technology). |
| 2 | **Immune to the comparison trap** | We compare upward; feeds show everyone's highlight reel → worse mood/self-esteem. | Festinger, social comparison (1954); Vogel et al. (2014); Hunt et al. (2018). |
| 3 | **Deeper focus / attention** | Fewer context-switches and notifications → longer sustained attention, "flow"/deep work. | Cal Newport, *Deep Work* / *Digital Minimalism*; attention-residue research (Mark). |
| 4 | **Present in real moments** | Not experiencing life through a lens/for-the-post; "phubbing" lowers enjoyment & connection. | Kushlev & Dunn; "iagnation"/phubbing studies. |
| 5 | **Fewer but deeper friendships** | Humans can only sustain ~5 intimate and ~150 casual bonds; thousands of "friends" dilute, not deepen. | Robin Dunbar (Dunbar's number). |
| 6 | **Comfortable being unseen** | Identity isn't outsourced to likes; doesn't need an audience to feel real. | Self-determination theory; contingent self-worth research. |
| 7 | **Protects their privacy** | Leaves fewer data trails; less surveilled and profiled. | Attention-economy / data-economy critiques. |
| 8 | **Lower FOMO & anxiety** | Not plugged into the live stream of everything they're "missing"; cutting use lowers anxiety/loneliness. | Przybylski et al. (2013, FOMO); Hunt et al. (2018). |
| 9 | **Boredom → creativity** | Unstimulated, wandering minds generate more ideas; constant scrolling erases the boredom that fuels creativity. | Mann & Cadman (2014); Sandi Mann, *The Upside of Downtime*. |

## On-screen numbers (StatCards)
- **~2.5 hrs/day** — average time on social media worldwide (DataReportal / GWI,
  2023–24; framed as "more than two hours a day"). Used to set the "habitat."
- **~5** — the number of truly close relationships humans tend to sustain (Dunbar's
  innermost layer).
- **30 min** — Hunt et al. (2018, U Penn): limiting social media to ~30 min/day
  **reduced loneliness and depression** over three weeks.

## Honest trade-offs (its own chapter — keeps it non-smug)
Offline people can be **out of the loop** on news, in-jokes, and events; social
coordination is harder; some communities, jobs, marginalized voices, and long-
distance bonds genuinely **depend** on these platforms. The healthy point is
**intentional use**, not purity. Opting out is a trade-off, not a trophy.

## Myth-bust (handled in VO)
Not **antisocial** (often the opposite — more in-person time), not **technophobes**,
not **missing out** on what matters, not **judging you**. Different diet, not a
higher moral plane.

## Disclaimer (also in metadata)
Education and curiosity, not judgment. Social media has real benefits, and using it
doesn't make you shallow any more than quitting makes you wise. This is a portrait
of tendencies, not a scorecard — the goal is using these tools on purpose, whatever
you choose.
