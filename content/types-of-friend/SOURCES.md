# Sources — Every Type of Friend Explained

The different roles friends play, why we need a variety of them, the science of how
friendships form and how many we can really hold, and why a few deep ones beat a huge
crowd. Warm, practical, evidence-informed. Not a diagnosis.

## Why friends matter (the big one)
- Friendship isn't a luxury — it's one of the strongest predictors of **health and
  happiness**. The **Harvard Study of Adult Development** (80+ years) found that the
  quality of our **relationships** is the single best predictor of a long, happy life —
  better than money, fame, or even cholesterol. Loneliness is as harmful as smoking by
  some measures; good friends are protective.

## Dunbar's circles — we hold friends in layers
- Anthropologist **Robin Dunbar** describes friendship as **nested layers**: roughly a
  **support clique of ~5** (your closest), a **sympathy group of ~15**, a **band of
  ~50**, and the famous **"Dunbar's number" of ~150** meaningful relationships total.
  Each layer takes **more time and emotional investment** to maintain, and the inner
  ones are necessarily **small**.

## Friendship takes time to form
- **Jeffrey Hall's research:** it takes roughly **50 hours** together to move from
  acquaintance to casual friend, about **90 hours** to become real friends, and over
  **200 hours** to become **close** friends. Friendship is built through **accumulated
  shared time**, not willed into being — which is why it's hard to make close friends
  as a busy adult.

## We need different friends for different needs
- No single person can be your **everything** (expecting that is a recipe for
  disappointment). Healthy social lives have **relational diversity** — different
  friends meet different needs: fun, depth, advice, history, novelty, support. A rich
  life usually means a **portfolio** of friendships, not one do-it-all best friend.

## The main types (stated in the video)
- **The ride-or-die** — the inner-circle friend who shows up in a crisis, no questions.
- **The cheerleader** — relentlessly encouraging; believes in you, lifts you up.
- **The mentor** — older/wiser, offers perspective and guidance.
- **The old friend** — knows your whole history; low-maintenance, picks up where you
  left off.
- **The work friend** — context-bound ally who makes daily life better (may or may not
  survive outside that context).
- **The fair-weather friend** — around for the good times, scarce in the hard ones.
- **The mirror / growth friend** — challenges you, tells you the hard truths, makes you
  better.
- **The acquaintance / weak tie** — light but genuinely valuable for mood, belonging,
  and opportunity (Granovetter's "strength of weak ties").
- **The adventure buddy** — the novelty-and-fun friend who gets you out of your rut.
- **The drainer** — the one-sided, depleting "friend"; a prompt to look at balance and
  boundaries.

## Quality over quantity
- **A few deep friendships beat a huge crowd of shallow ones.** Research consistently
  links wellbeing to the **quality and closeness** of friendships, not the headcount.
  One or two people who truly know you matter more than hundreds of followers.

## Friendships change — and that's okay
- It's normal for friendships to **fade, drift, or end** as lives diverge; studies
  suggest we **turn over** a large share of our network every several years. A
  friendship ending isn't always a failure — sometimes people are "**friends for a
  season**," and outgrowing one can be healthy.

## Honest takeaways (stated in the video)
- Value the **variety** — don't ask one friend to be all of them.
- **Invest the hours** — closeness is built through time and showing up.
- Protect the **inner circle** — a few deep ties matter most.
- Notice the **drainers**, and tend the **reciprocal** ones.
- And be the kind of friend you'd want to have. **Not a diagnosis** — struggling with
  friendship is common and workable, and worth real support if loneliness runs deep.
