## Description
Someone lights up and says hello — and you know the face, you remember their dog, you
know exactly where you met. But their name? Completely, utterly gone. If this happens
to you constantly, relax: it's one of the most universal quirks of human memory, and a
sign your brain is working exactly as designed.

This is the science of faces vs names:

0:00 "Heyyy... you"
Two different jobs (recognition vs recall) · faces get special hardware (the fusiform
face area) · names mean nothing (arbitrary labels) · the Baker paradox (same word,
different fate) · you weren't even listening (it was never stored) · and four steps to
actually remember names.

🧠 You're not bad with names — you have a brain lovingly engineered over millions of
years to be a world-class face expert, that never got the upgrade for arbitrary verbal
labels. To remember a name, give it the hooks it's missing: attend, repeat, associate,
and use it. And if all else fails, just ask — it's a compliment.

⚠️ Everyday name-blanking is normal (and a touch more common with age). Not a
diagnosis — but a marked, worsening decline in memory is worth a chat with your doctor.
