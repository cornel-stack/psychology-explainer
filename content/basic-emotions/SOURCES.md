# Sources — Every Emotion Explained

A tour of the **basic emotions** — what they are, what each one is *for*, and how
they combine. Framed around **Robert Plutchik's wheel of emotions** (eight
primary emotions in opposing pairs, which blend into richer "dyads") and **Paul
Ekman's** work on basic, cross-culturally recognised emotions.

Distinct from Video #1 ("Every Feeling You Can't Name") — that was about obscure,
untranslatable *feelings*; this is the science of the core, universal emotions and
their function.

## The eight (Plutchik's primaries, in opposite pairs)
- **Joy ↔ Sadness**
- **Trust ↔ Disgust**
- **Fear ↔ Anger**
- **Surprise ↔ Anticipation**

## Each emotion's job (functional / evolutionary view)
- **Fear** — detects threat; triggers fight-flight-freeze; protects you.
- **Anger** — responds to boundary violation / injustice; mobilises to defend.
- **Sadness** — processes loss; signals need; draws support and prompts reflection.
- **Joy** — rewards and signals "do more of this"; builds bonds (broaden-and-build,
  Fredrickson).
- **Disgust** — originally avoids contamination/toxins; extends to moral disgust.
- **Surprise** — resets attention to the unexpected; primes rapid learning.
- **Anticipation** — orients to the future; drives planning and preparation.
- **Trust** — enables cooperation, bonding and safety with others.

## Plutchik's blends (primary dyads)
- Joy + Trust = **Love**
- Joy + Anticipation = **Optimism**
- Fear + Surprise = **Alarm / Awe**
- Sadness + Disgust = **Remorse**
- (Anger + Anticipation = Aggressiveness; Trust + Fear = Submission; etc.)

## Working with emotions (the practical core)
- **Emotions are signals, not commands** — information about your situation, not
  orders you must obey. (Appraisal theory; the gap between feeling and acting.)
- **Name it to tame it** — "affect labelling" reduces amygdala reactivity
  (Lieberman et al.); putting feelings into words calms them.
- **Allow, don't suppress** — avoided emotions tend to intensify and leak; accepting
  and feeling them through is healthier than bottling or exploding.
- **Intensity varies** — each emotion runs from mild to intense (annoyance →
  rage); same emotion, different volume.

## Honest framing (stated in the video)
- **No emotion is "bad."** Even fear, anger, sadness and disgust are useful signals
  — the goal is to understand and work *with* them, not banish them.
- The basic-emotions model is a **useful map, debated at the edges** (how many
  "basic" emotions there are, and how culturally universal, is still argued —
  e.g. constructed-emotion theory, Barrett). Presented as a helpful lens.
- Psycho-education, not therapy or diagnosis; if emotions feel overwhelming or
  numb for a long time, that's worth talking to a professional about.
