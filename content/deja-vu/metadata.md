## Description
You walk into a room you've never visited, in a city you've never been — and
suddenly, a chill: you've been here before. You know this moment. And yet you also
know that's impossible. Welcome to one of the strangest glitches in the human mind.

This is the real neuroscience of déjà vu:

0:00 The uncanny chill
What it actually is (a feeling at war with a fact) · who gets it (most of us,
especially the young) · two memory systems (familiarity vs recollection) · the
glitch (familiar, but no memory) · maybe your brain is fine (it's fact-checking
itself) · the layout trick (a forgotten shape returns) · where it lives (the temporal
lobe) · jamais vu (the opposite) · and the premonition illusion.

✨ Déjà vu may be the sound of your brain catching its own mistake — a sign your
memory is working *well*. Not a premonition, past life, or warning. (Very frequent,
intense déjà vu with zoning out, odd smells or lost time can rarely signal a
temporal-lobe issue — worth mentioning to a doctor.)
